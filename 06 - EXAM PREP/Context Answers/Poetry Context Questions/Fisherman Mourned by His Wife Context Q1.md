
"My face was wan while telling you and voices fell low,
and you seemed full of guilt and not to know
whether to repent or rejoice over the situation
You nodded at the ground and went to sea,
But soon I was to you more than God or temptation, and so were you to me"


This extraction is important because it represents to a flashback and a stream of consciousness style. The sensitive moment when the wife informs her husband that she is pregnant, through this memory the poet develops both characterization and the theme of love maturing from uncertainty into deep mutual dependence.

At this point in the poem, the speaker recalls, how nervous and physically weak she felt "My face was wan... voices fell low" these details reveals her anxiety and vulnerability. She is unsure of her husband's reaction, which shows that at this early stage of their marriage they are still somewhat polite strangers rather than emotionally close partners. This reflects the cultural context of traditional Sri Lankan society where open discussion of pregnancy and intimate matters are often restrained. The understated tone effectively captures this social awkwardness without becoming sentimental.

The husband's reaction is presented through careful visual imagery, the wife observes that he seemed unsure "whether to repent or rejoice over the situation" the juxtaposition of the opposite emotion; reject and rejoice, highlights his confusion and inexperience. It also suggests, the immature emotional state of both partners at this stage. His shy response is further emphasized when he "nodded at the ground and went to sea". His inability to look directly at his wife reflects embracement, modesty and emotional distance.

However, the final two lines mark a powerful transformation, the statement "you were to me more than God or temptation" shows how their relationship quickly deepens after this moment. The hyperbolic comparison suggests that, their love becomes all consuming central to their lives. What begins as an arranged or socially convenient marriage gradually develops into genuine emotional bonding, trust and respect.  The growth is especially significant because the poem is an elegy; the wife is now mourning her husband, who once became her greatest source of support.

Fernando's lyrical lines, and controlled diction maintain an understated, yet deeply moving tone. The extract efficiently presents the evolution of a traditional marriage into a relationship of profound love and mutual dependence. Thus, this excerpt captures the couples movements from awkward distance to intimate unity, while also reflecting cultural values, emotional maturity and the quiet strength of marital love.

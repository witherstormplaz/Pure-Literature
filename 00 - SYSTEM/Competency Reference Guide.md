## PART 1 - LITERARY TEXT ANALYSIS

### COMPETENCY 1: Identifies and analyses themes, literary techniques, and the use of language in creating different levels of meaning

**What this means**:
- Identify the main themes in a text
- Understand how themes are developed through the text
- Recognize literary techniques (metaphor, simile, personification, alliteration, etc.)
- Analyze how language creates meaning
- Understand how techniques contribute to theme development
- Evaluate the overall effect of language and techniques

**Action verbs**: Identify, analyze, understand, become aware, recognize, examine, evaluate, compare

**What to include in notes**:
- List of all themes with evidence
- Detailed analysis of each theme's development
- Complete list of literary techniques with examples
- Analysis of language choices (diction, tone, syntax)
- How techniques support theme
- Overall evaluation of effectiveness

**Question types you'll see**:
- "Identify the main themes in this text"
- "How does the poet use language to develop the theme of [X]?"
- "Analyze the literary techniques in this passage"
- "Evaluate the effectiveness of the poet's use of language"
- "Compare how themes are developed in texts X and Y"

**Example from syllabus texts**:
- Theme of poverty in "Nectar in a Sieve" developed through imagery of land/harvest
- Use of animal imagery in "Othello" to dehumanize characters
- Metaphor in "Design" - spider/heal-all/moth as symbols

### COMPETENCY 2: Recognizes the main features of prose and poetry

**What this means**:
- Understand the physical and structural differences between prose and poetry
- Identify features unique to each genre
- Recognize why an author chose prose vs poetry for their work
- Observe how these features work within each text

**Action verbs**: Recognize, identify, observe, differentiate, respond

**Prose Features**:
- Organized in paragraphs and sentences
- Follows normal grammatical structure
- Uses prose rhythm (not metrical patterns)
- Narrative techniques: POV, narrative voice, dialogue
- Continuous text without line breaks

**Poetry Features**:
- Organized in lines and stanzas
- May or may not follow standard grammar
- Uses rhythm, meter, rhyme schemes
- Visual/spatial arrangement on page matters
- Line breaks are meaningful
- Compression of language
- Often uses imagery more densely

**What to include in notes**:
- Identification of text type
- Specific prose/poetry features present
- How these features work in the text
- Why the form suits the content

**Question types you'll see**:
- "How does this text demonstrate features of poetry/prose?"
- "Differentiate between the prose and poetry extracts provided"
- "Why is poetry an appropriate form for this content?"

**Example from syllabus**:
- "Fisherman mourned by his wife" (poetry): free verse, line breaks create pauses, visual shape on page
- "Cat in the Rain" (prose): paragraph structure, narrative voice, dialogue conventions
- Comparing Othello (drama - uses prose AND verse differently for different characters/situations)

### COMPETENCY 3: Recognizes the basic characteristics of different literary genres

**What this means**:
- Know the defining features of major genres (poetry, drama, short story, novel)
- Recognize sub-genres within these (tragedy, sonnet, etc.)
- Trace how content displays genre features
- Examine unique features in specific texts
- Analyze how features enrich or weaken the text

**Action verbs**: Know, recognize, relate, trace, examine, demonstrate

**Genre Features**:

**POETRY**
- Genres: Sonnet, free verse, dramatic monologue, ode, etc.
- Features: Compression, musicality, imagery-density, economy of language

**DRAMA**
- Genres: Tragedy, comedy, tragicomedy
- Features: Dialogue, stage directions, acts/scenes, dramatic irony, soliloquy, aside
- Designed for performance

**SHORT STORY**
- Features: Brevity, economy, limited scope
- Single effect/unified impression
- Focus: Limited characters, single time period, limited setting
- Often has epiphany or revelation

**NOVEL**
- Features: Length allows for development over time
- Multiple plot lines and subplots
- Extensive character development
- Detailed setting and context
- Can cover long time periods

**What to include in notes**:
- Genre classification (specific sub-genre if applicable)
- List of genre-specific features present
- How these features function in this particular text
- Unique features or variations

**Question types you'll see**:
- "What genre characteristics does this text demonstrate?"
- "How does this text follow/subvert conventions of [genre]?"
- "Examine the unique features of this text in relation to its genre"

**Example from syllabus**:
- "Sonnet 73" (Shakespeare): 14 lines, ABAB CDCD EFEF GG rhyme scheme, iambic pentameter, volta
- "Othello": Shakespearean tragedy - tragic hero, hamartia, catharsis, 5-act structure
- "Cat in the Rain": Short story - single effect (isolation/desire), limited characters, brief time span

### COMPETENCY 4: Makes inferences correctly, and reads insightfully

**What this means**:
- Understand what is implied but not directly stated
- Read "between the lines"
- Identify significance of implied meanings
- Interpret unstated meanings correctly
- Make plausible inferences based on textual evidence
- Improve quality of thought through additional reading

**Action verbs**: Observe, identify, interpret, improve

**Skills needed**:
- Close reading
- Attention to detail
- Understanding of context
- Ability to draw logical conclusions
- Recognition of subtlety and nuance

**What to look for**:
- Subtext in dialogue
- Symbolic meanings
- What characters don't say
- Gaps and silences in narrative
- Implications of imagery
- Cultural/contextual references

**What to include in notes**:
- Section on "Unstated/Implied Meanings"
- Analysis of subtext
- Interpretation of symbolic elements
- What can be inferred about characters, situations, themes
- Supporting evidence for inferences

**Question types you'll see**:
- "What is implied by [passage/line]?"
- "What can we infer about [character/situation] from this scene?"
- "Interpret the unstated meanings in [text]"
- "What significance does [detail] have?"

**Example from syllabus**:
- In "Cat in the Rain": Wife's desire for cat implies larger desires (stability, motherhood, attention)
- In "Othello": Iago's asides reveal his true thoughts vs. what he says to others
- In "The Chimney Sweeper": Child's acceptance of exploitation implies societal normalization of child labor

### COMPETENCY 5: Responds to and engages with the texts in relation to imagery; ideas, emotions, attitudes, context etc.

**What this means**:
- Understand the concept of imagery
- Recognize different types of imagery (sensory, literary devices)
- Analyze imagery's relationship to meaning
- Evaluate significance and relevance of imagery
- Relate text to socio-cultural context through imagery

**Action verbs**: Understand, know, recognize, respond, analyze, evaluate, relate

**Types of Imagery**:

**A) SENSORY IMAGERY**:
- Visual (sight)
- Auditory (sound)
- Olfactory (smell)
- Gustatory (taste)
- Tactile (touch)

**B) LITERARY DEVICES**:
- Simile (explicit comparison using "like"/"as")
- Metaphor (implicit comparison)
- Symbol (object representing abstract idea)
- Personification (human qualities to non-human)
- Alliteration, assonance, onomatopoeia

**What to include in notes**:
- Complete imagery analysis section
- Type of imagery (sensory AND devices)
- Specific examples with line numbers/references
- Effect created by the imagery
- How imagery relates to theme
- How imagery reveals emotions/attitudes
- Connection to socio-cultural context

**Question types you'll see**:
- "Analyze the imagery in this text"
- "What effect does the imagery of [X] create?"
- "How does the poet use imagery to convey [emotion/idea]?"
- "Evaluate the significance of imagery in relation to the text's context"
- "Respond to the use of sensory imagery in this passage"

**Example from syllabus**:
- "Explosion" (Larkin): Visual imagery of miners, light, contrast between underground/sky
- "Othello": Animal imagery for Othello shifts from noble (lion) to base (goat, monkey)
- "Nectar in a Sieve": Natural imagery (seasons, harvest) tied to cycles of hope/despair

### COMPETENCY 6: Develops a personal response to a text through critical thinking and analysis

**What this means**:
- Develop YOUR OWN thoughtful response to texts
- Use critical thinking skills
- Analyze texts through various parameters
- Relate ideas/emotions to your personal experience/worldview
- Scrutinize texts in relation to reality/understanding
- Improve quality of thought
- Evaluate quality of writing

**Action verbs**: Use, analyze, relate, scrutinize, improve, evaluate

**This is the highest-level competency** - requires:
- All skills from Competencies 1-5
- PLUS your own critical perspective
- PLUS ability to evaluate and judge

**What to include in notes**:
- Personal response section
- Critical perspectives section
- Your evaluation of the text's quality
- How text relates to your worldview/experience
- Critical analysis beyond just identifying features
- Quality assessment with justification

**Question types you'll see**:
- "What is your personal response to this text?"
- "How does this text relate to your own experience/understanding?"
- "Evaluate the quality of writing in this text"
- "Critically analyze how this text explores [theme]"
- "To what extent do you agree with [interpretation]?"

**Example from syllabus**:
- Personal response to "Phenomenal Woman": How does this empower/challenge you?
- Critical analysis of "Othello": Is it racist? Is it a critique of racism? Both?
- Evaluation of "Nectar in a Sieve": Does Markandaya romanticize poverty or critique it?

## PART 2 - LANGUAGE & WRITING SKILLS

### COMPETENCY 7: Presents a personal critical response to the literary texts using appropriate conventions of academic/formal essay writing

**What this means**:
- Write essays that respond to literary texts
- Use appropriate essay structure
- Cite textual evidence correctly
- Support analysis with references
- Use academic/formal writing conventions

**Action verbs**: Use, cite, refer

**Essay Components**:
- Clear introduction with thesis
- Body paragraphs with topic sentences
- Textual evidence (quotes, references)
- Analysis of evidence
- Logical organization
- Formal academic tone
- Proper citations
- Strong conclusion

**What to practice**:
- Essay structure
- Incorporating quotes
- Analyzing (not just summarizing)
- Formal register
- Avoiding personal pronouns (unless asked for personal response)
- Logical paragraph progression

## HOW COMPETENCIES MAP TO EXAM QUESTIONS

### Typical Question Patterns:

**Context Questions** (usually C1, C4, C5):
- "What is the theme of this passage?"
- "Identify the literary techniques used"
- "What is the significance of [image/symbol]?"

**Analysis Questions** (usually C1, C3, C4, C5, C6):
- "Analyze how the poet develops the theme of [X]"
- "How does this text demonstrate characteristics of [genre]?"
- "Critically evaluate this text"

**Comparison Questions** (C1, C6):
- "Compare how themes are developed in texts X and Y"
- "Compare the use of imagery in these two poems"

**Personal Response Questions** (C6):
- "What is your personal response to this text?"
- "How does this text relate to your experience?"
- "Evaluate the quality of this writing"

**Part 2 Questions** (C7-C13):
- Essay writing on given topic
- Précis/summary of passage
- Unseen text analysis

## USING THIS IN YOUR NOTES

For every text you study, your note should show coverage of relevant competencies.

**Minimum for every text**:
- C1 (themes & techniques) ✓
- C2 or C3 (form/genre) ✓
- C4 (inference) ✓
- C5 (imagery) ✓
- C6 (critical response) ✓

Use the checklist at the top of each note template to ensure you've covered everything.

## QUICK COMPETENCY CHECKLIST

When studying a text, ask yourself:

- [ ] Have I identified all major themes? (C1)
- [ ] Have I analyzed all literary techniques? (C1)
- [ ] Have I identified prose/poetry features? (C2)
- [ ] Have I analyzed genre characteristics? (C3)
- [ ] Have I explored unstated meanings? (C4)
- [ ] Have I done complete imagery analysis? (C5)
- [ ] Have I written my personal critical response? (C6)

If you can check all these boxes, your note is complete.

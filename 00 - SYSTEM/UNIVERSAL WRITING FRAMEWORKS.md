## PART A: CONTEXT QUESTION FRAMEWORK

**Time: 20 minutes | Words: 200-250 | Marks: 8**

### THE UNIVERSAL STRUCTURE

```
[IDENTIFICATION - 2-3 sentences]

This is [speaker/character] speaking to [whom] in [author's] [text]. 
This occurs [when/where in the text].

At this point in the [play/novel/story/poem], [brief context of what's happening].

[IMMEDIATE CONTEXT - 3-4 sentences]

Before this moment, [what happened immediately before].

In this passage, [character/speaker] [what they're doing/saying in the passage].
After this, [what happens next].

This moment is crucial because [why this moment matters to plot].

[SIGNIFICANCE - 4-5 sentences]

The passage is significant to the [play/novel/story/poem] as a whole because 
[connect to major theme 1].

It also reveals [character insight/development].

[Author's] use of [specific technique/language/imagery from passage] demonstrates 
[what effect this creates].

This connects to the broader theme of [major theme 2] by showing [how].

[ANALYSIS OF LANGUAGE - 2-3 sentences]
The [metaphor/imagery/diction/tone] in "[quote specific phrase from passage]" 
[what it suggests/reveals/symbolizes].
[Another technique] creates [what effect].
This passage thus [final statement of significance].
```

### EXAMPLE (FILLED IN)

**Passage**: "You taught me language, and my profit on't / Is I know how to curse" (The Tempest)

**Answer using framework**:

> This is Caliban speaking to Prospero in Shakespeare's _The Tempest_. This occurs early in the play during a confrontation on Prospero's island. At this point, Prospero is accusing Caliban of ingratitude for the education he provided.
> 
> Before this moment, Prospero has asserted his authority over Caliban. In this passage, Caliban bitterly responds that the only benefit ("profit") he gained from learning Prospero's language is the ability to curse his enslaver. After this, the colonial power dynamic becomes a central tension in the play. This moment is crucial because it establishes Caliban's awareness of his subjugation.
> 
> The passage is significant to the play as a whole because it encapsulates the colonial relationship between colonizer and colonized. It also reveals Caliban's intelligence and self-awareness - he understands exactly how he's been exploited. Shakespeare's use of the ironic term "profit" (an economic word) demonstrates how colonial education serves the colonizer's interests, not the colonized. This connects to the broader theme of power and oppression by showing that even supposed "gifts" from the oppressor are tools of control.
> 
> The bitter tone in "my profit on't / Is I know how to curse" suggests Caliban's complete rejection of Prospero's narrative of benevolence. The juxtaposition of "language" (civilization) with "curse" (resistance) creates a powerful reversal of colonial expectations. This passage thus establishes language itself as both a weapon of oppression and a tool of resistance.

**Word count**: ~245 words | **Time**: 18 minutes

## FRAMEWORK 1: THEME QUESTION

**"How does [author] explore/portray [theme]?"**

### UNIVERSAL STRUCTURE

```
INTRODUCTION (50-75 words):
In [text], [author] explores [theme] as [your thesis - what author says about theme].
Through [method 1], [method 2], and [method 3], the text reveals [main insight about theme].
This essay will examine how [restate your approach to answering].

BODY PARAGRAPH 1 (100-125 words):
[Author's] first approach to [theme] is through [aspect 1].
[Topic sentence about first point].
This is evident when [character/narrator] [action/statement], shown in the [line/passage] 
"[QUOTE]."
This [quote/passage] demonstrates [analysis of what quote shows about theme].
[Author's] use of [specific technique] creates [what effect].
Furthermore, [deeper analysis connecting to theme].
This reveals that [theme] is [what characteristic/aspect].

BODY PARAGRAPH 2 (100-125 words):
[Similar structure to Paragraph 1, but different aspect of theme]
Moreover/In addition/Furthermore, [author] explores [theme] through [aspect 2].
[Evidence and analysis following same pattern]

BODY PARAGRAPH 3 (75-100 words) [OPTIONAL - use if time]:
[Similar structure, third aspect if needed]

CONCLUSION (50-75 words):
Thus, [author's] exploration of [theme] in [text] is [your final assessment].
Through [recap methods briefly], the text demonstrates that [main insight about theme].
Ultimately, [author's] treatment of [theme] [why significant/what it reveals/impact on reader].
```

### EXAMPLE (FILLED IN)

**Question**: "How does Markandaya explore poverty in _Nectar in a Sieve_?"

> In _Nectar in a Sieve_, Markandaya explores poverty as both a destructive force and a test of human resilience. Through Rukmani's narration, the symbolic connection to land, and the contrast between traditional and modern economic systems, the text reveals that poverty strips dignity while simultaneously demonstrating the endurance of the human spirit. This essay will examine how Markandaya portrays poverty's physical, emotional, and social dimensions.
> 
> Markandaya's first approach to poverty is through its visceral, physical reality. Rukmani's understated narration reveals suffering through simple statements like "We were hungry" rather than elaborate descriptions. This is evident when the family faces starvation, shown in the passage "There were days when we ate grass and weeds and whatever we could find." This quote demonstrates poverty's reduction of humans to survival mode, where dignity becomes a luxury. Markandaya's use of simple, direct prose creates an unflinching portrayal of deprivation. Furthermore, the repetitive cycle of hardship—drought, then flood, then famine—shows poverty as relentless rather than temporary. This reveals that poverty is a structural condition, not merely bad luck.
> 
> Moreover, Markandaya explores poverty through the land's symbolic significance. Rukmani's connection to her land represents both hope and vulnerability. When she says "The land is ours. It will not fail us," the irony becomes apparent as the land repeatedly does fail them through natural disasters and economic exploitation. The tannery's arrival destroys their agricultural livelihood, forcing them into a market economy where they're powerless. This juxtaposition between traditional subsistence and modern capitalism exposes poverty as partly created by systemic forces beyond individual control.
> 
> Thus, Markandaya's exploration of poverty in _Nectar in a Sieve_ is both unflinching and compassionate. Through Rukmani's voice, symbolic land imagery, and structural economic critique, the text demonstrates that poverty dehumanizes while simultaneously revealing human dignity through resilience. Ultimately, Markandaya's treatment of poverty challenges readers to see beyond individual suffering to systemic injustice, making the novel both a personal story and social commentary.

**Word count**: ~380 words

## FRAMEWORK 2: CHARACTER QUESTION

**"Analyze the character of [X]" or "How does [author] present [character]?"**

### UNIVERSAL STRUCTURE

```
INTRODUCTION:
In [text], [character] is presented as [your thesis about character].
[Author's] characterization reveals [what this character represents/embodies].
Through [method 1], [method 2], and [method 3], we see [character's main qualities/role].

BODY PARAGRAPH 1 - Actions:
[Character's] actions demonstrate [characteristic/trait].
[Specific action/scene where this is shown].
For instance, when [character] [action], stating/shown in "[QUOTE]."
This action reveals [analysis of motivation/significance].
[Author's technique in presenting this].
This establishes [character] as [trait/role].

BODY PARAGRAPH 2 - Relationships/Development:
[Character's] relationship with [other character(s)] further illuminates [aspect].
[How they interact, evidence, quote].
[Analysis of what this reveals].
[How character changes/develops or remains static].

BODY PARAGRAPH 3 - Symbolic/Thematic Role:
Beyond individual characterization, [character] represents [larger idea/theme].
[Evidence of symbolic function].
[What character reveals about author's message].

CONCLUSION:
Therefore, [author's] presentation of [character] creates [what kind of character].
Through [methods], we understand [character] as [assessment].
This characterization serves to [purpose in text/theme].
```

### UNIVERSAL STRUCTURE

```

[Author] achieves [effect/goal] through [your thesis - main methods].

This essay will examine [author's] use of [method 1], [method 2], and [method 3].

BODY PARAGRAPH 1 - Technique/Method 1:
[Author's] primary technique is [technique 1].
[Explanation of technique].
This is evident in [example/scene/passage]: "[QUOTE]."
This [achieves what effect/reveals what].
[Deeper analysis of how technique works].
[Effect on reader/purpose].

BODY PARAGRAPH 2 - Technique/Method 2:
Additionally/Furthermore, [author] employs [technique 2].
[Same pattern - example, quote, analysis].

BODY PARAGRAPH 3 - Technique/Method 3:
[Same pattern for third technique if needed]

CONCLUSION:
Thus, [author] achieves [effect] through [recap techniques].
These methods work together to [combined effect].
The result is [overall achievement/impact].
```

## FRAMEWORK 4: AGREE/DISAGREE QUESTION

**"Do you agree with [critical statement]?"**

### UNIVERSAL STRUCTURE (ALWAYS USE THIS FOR "AGREE?" QUESTIONS)

```
INTRODUCTION:
The statement that "[quote the statement]" presents [a compelling/a problematic/a partial] view of [text].
While [acknowledge what's true in statement], this essay will argue that [your nuanced position].
[Text] is more complex than this statement suggests because [your main counter-argument].

BODY PARAGRAPH 1 - Points of Agreement:
To some extent, the statement is accurate.
[Evidence that supports the statement].
[Example/quote that seems to confirm it].
[Analysis showing why this reading is possible].

BODY PARAGRAPH 2 - Points of Disagreement:
However, this view oversimplifies/ignores [what it misses].
[Counter-evidence that challenges the statement].
[Example/quote that contradicts or complicates it].
[Analysis of why statement is limited/wrong].

BODY PARAGRAPH 3 - Nuanced Synthesis:
A more nuanced reading reveals [your balanced position].
[How truth lies between extremes].
[What a better understanding would be].
[Evidence for this more complex view].

CONCLUSION:
Therefore, while [partial agreement], the statement fails to account for [what it misses].
[Text] demonstrates not [simplistic reading] but rather [more complex reality].
[Author's] treatment is [your final assessment].
```

### EXAMPLE (FILLED IN)

**Question**: "_Nectar in a Sieve_ denies poor people courage and agency - they all end up as victims. Do you agree?"

> The statement that "Nectar in a Sieve denies poor people courage and agency - they all end up as victims" presents a partial view of Markandaya's novel. While the text certainly depicts the devastating effects of poverty and shows characters suffering from forces beyond their control, this essay will argue that the novel simultaneously celebrates resilience and resistance. _Nectar in a Sieve_ is more complex than this statement suggests because Rukmani repeatedly demonstrates agency even within severe constraints.
> 
> To some extent, the statement is accurate. The novel shows poverty as stripping away choices and reducing characters to survival mode. Rukmani's family suffers repeated tragedies—starvation, her daughter Ira's prostitution, her sons' deaths—without ability to prevent them. When Nathan says "We are at the mercy of the land," it captures their powerlessness against natural disasters and economic exploitation. The ending, with Nathan's death and Rukmani alone, could support a reading of ultimate victimhood. These examples show how poverty does limit agency.
> 
> However, this view oversimplifies Rukmani's consistent acts of resistance and courage. When she secretly saves money grain by grain despite Nathan's prohibition, she exercises agency. When she seeks Kenny's medical help repeatedly, risking community judgment, she chooses action over passivity. Her statement "I am not one to accept fate without a struggle" directly contradicts the claim that she's merely a victim. Markandaya presents Rukmani as repeatedly making difficult choices—to work, to seek help, to continue—within impossible circumstances. Confusing constrained choices with no choices misreads the novel.
> 
> A more nuanced reading reveals that _Nectar in a Sieve_ portrays both structural oppression and human agency coexisting. Rukmani is neither all-powerful nor completely powerless. She cannot change the economic system, but she can decide how to respond to it. The novel's tragedy lies not in characters lacking courage but in their courage being insufficient against systemic forces. This makes the novel more powerful social critique than simple victimization narrative.
> 
> Therefore, while the novel does show how poverty limits lives, the statement fails to account for the persistent agency and resilience Markandaya portrays. _Nectar in a Sieve_ demonstrates not passive victimhood but active survival against overwhelming odds. Markandaya's treatment is both realistic about oppression and celebratory of human endurance.

## FRAMEWORK 5: COMPARISON/MULTI-TEXT QUESTION

**For Poetry (3+ poems) or Short Stories (2-3 stories)**

**"Compare how [theme/element] is treated in [X poems/stories]"**

### UNIVERSAL STRUCTURE

```
INTRODUCTION:
[Theme/element] is explored differently across [texts listed].
While all texts address [common element], they differ in [main point of comparison].
This essay will examine how [Text A], [Text B], and [Text C] approach [theme/element].

BODY PARAGRAPH 1 - Text A:
[Text A] by [author] presents [theme/element] as [characterization].
[Evidence/quote from Text A].
[Analysis of technique/approach in Text A].
[What this reveals about theme].

BODY PARAGRAPH 2 - Text B Compared to A:
In contrast/Similarly, [Text B] by [author] treats [theme/element] as [characterization].
[Evidence/quote from Text B].
Unlike/Like [Text A], which [recap A's approach], [Text B] [how B differs/similar].
[Analysis of B's unique approach].

BODY PARAGRAPH 3 - Text C in Relation to A and B:
[Text C] offers yet another/a middle perspective on [theme].
[Evidence/quote from Text C].
Where [Text A] emphasizes [what] and [Text B] emphasizes [what], [Text C] [what it does].
[Analysis of how C completes the picture].

CONCLUSION:
Therefore, these texts reveal [theme's] complexity through diverse treatments.
[Text A] shows [summary], [Text B] shows [summary], and [Text C] shows [summary].
Together, they demonstrate that [theme] [overall insight from comparison].
```

### EXAMPLE (FILLED IN)

**Question**: "Examine how poets engage with the passage of time in THREE poems."

> The passage of time is explored differently across Rossetti's "Remember," Keats's "Ode to a Nightingale," and Shakespeare's "Sonnet 73." While all poems address mortality and time's effects, they differ in their responses—resistance, transcendence, and acceptance respectively. This essay will examine how these three poems approach time's inevitability.
> 
> "Remember" by Christina Rossetti presents time as an enemy to fight through memory. The speaker instructs her beloved to "Remember me when I am gone away," attempting to extend her presence beyond death through remembrance. The imperative mood throughout ("Remember," "do not grieve") shows the speaker trying to control time's effects through sheer will. However, the volta shifts to "Better by far you should forget and smile," revealing acceptance that memory itself causes pain. This reveals time as ultimately undefeatable—even memory cannot preserve what's lost.
> 
> In contrast, "Ode to a Nightingale" by John Keats treats time through the lens of art's immortality. Unlike Rossetti's mortal speaker trying to be remembered, Keats juxtaposes human mortality with the nightingale's eternal song: "Thou wast not born for death, immortal Bird!" The bird represents art that transcends time—the same song heard by "emperor and clown" across ages. Where Rossetti accepts time's victory, Keats suggests art achieves what humans cannot—permanence. The contrast between "the weariness, the fever, and the fret" of human life and the nightingale's timeless beauty creates tension between temporal suffering and eternal art.
> 
> "Sonnet 73" offers yet another perspective: acceptance and even beauty in time's passage. Where Rossetti resists time and Keats seeks to transcend it, Shakespeare embraces aging: "That time of year thou mayst in me behold." The autumn imagery ("yellow leaves"), twilight, and dying fire create poignant beauty rather than tragic loss. The final couplet—"This thou perceiv'st, which makes thy love more strong, / To love that well which thou must leave ere long"—suggests time's brevity intensifies rather than diminishes love. This transforms time from enemy to teacher.
> 
> Therefore, these texts reveal time's complexity through diverse treatments. Rossetti shows the futility yet necessity of resistance, Keats shows the possibility of transcendence through art, and Shakespeare shows acceptance as wisdom. Together, they demonstrate that the passage of time is literature's eternal subject precisely because it admits no single answer.

**Word count**: ~385 words

## MASTER PARAGRAPH STRUCTURE

**Use this internal structure for EVERY body paragraph in Part B**

```
[TOPIC SENTENCE] - What this paragraph proves

[CONTEXT/EXPLANATION] - Brief setup for evidence

[EVIDENCE/QUOTE] - "[Quote]" or specific reference

[ANALYSIS] - What this evidence shows/means

[TECHNIQUE] - How author achieves this effect

[LINK TO QUESTION] - How this proves your thesis

[TRANSITION TO NEXT PARAGRAPH] - Furthermore/Moreover/In contrast
```

**Every paragraph follows this pattern = automatic coherent structure.**

### Why This Structure Works:

1. **Text-Type Organization** - Your brain already thinks in "poems vs drama vs short stories"
2. **Flat Poetry Folder** - You have 27 poems, keeping them in one searchable folder with clear naming is better than sub-folders
3. **Othello & Nectar Get Sub-folders** - These are long, complex texts that need multiple breakdown notes
4. **Part 2 Separate** - It's a different skill set, keep it isolated
5. **Cross-Text Connections** - For when you're comparing themes across texts (huge for Competency 6)

5. Plan next week's focus

#### **HOUR 2: Deep Work** (Next hour in your sequence)

This is your **consolidation hour**:

**Option A: Expand Notes**
- Take the rough analysis from Hour 1
- Add critical perspectives
- Make connections to other texts
- Add exam-relevant quotations
- Fill in any gaps

**Option B: Create Cross-Text Notes**
- If you've been studying multiple poems on similar themes
- Create/update notes in "07 - CROSS-TEXT CONNECTIONS"
- Map how different texts handle the same theme differently
- This is GOLD for Competency 6 (critical thinking)

**Option C: Part 2 Practice**
- If you're feeling saturated with Part 1
- Switch to Part 2 essay practice
- Write for 45 minutes
- Leave 15 minutes to review using your structures

---

#### **HOUR 3: Active Recall & Spaced Repetition** (Final hour)

This is your **solidification hour**:

**25 minutes**: Quiz yourself
- Pick random past paper questions
- Try to answer WITHOUT looking at notes first
- Check your answer against your notes
- Identify gaps

**25 minutes**: Memory consolidation
- Pick a text you studied 2-3 weeks ago
- Review its main note
- Can you still recall themes, quotes, analysis?
- Update the Review Log with today's date

**10 minutes**: Next-day prep
- Open tomorrow's notes
- Skim what you'll study
- Prime your brain for tomorrow's work

### Migration Strategy (DO THIS SYSTEMATICALLY):

#### **STEP 1: Export from NotebookLM** (1-2 days)

For each poem you have complete notes on:
1. Open the NotebookLM note
2. Copy ALL text content
3. Save each as a separate text file on your computer
4. Name them clearly: `The_Chimney_Sweeper_Blake.txt`
5. If there are audio notes you want to keep, download them too

#### **STEP 2: Restructure in Obsidian** (1 week, poem by poem)

For each poem:
1. Create a new note in Obsidian using the Poetry Template
2. Paste your NotebookLM content
3. **DON'T JUST DUMP IT** - reorganize it to fit the template sections:
   - Form & Structure section
   - Themes section
   - Language Techniques section
   - Imagery section
   - Critical thinking section
4. Add the metadata at top (competencies, term, status)
5. Fill in the competency checklist based on what's covered
6. Add any context/analysis questions you have
7. Add cross-links to related poems

**Time estimate**: 20-30 minutes per poem to restructure properly

#### **STEP 3: Enhance** (Ongoing)

Once your poems are in Obsidian:
1. Add connections between poems
2. Create theme-based notes in Cross-Text Connections folder
3. Add exam question appearances
4. Link to past paper questions

## FINAL NOTES

### Principles for Success:

1. **Consistency over Intensity**: 3 hours every night beats 10 hours one night and nothing for 3 days

2. **System over Memory**: Trust your Obsidian system to remember everything; your brain is for understanding, not storage

3. **Active Recall over Passive Review**: Test yourself, don't just re-read

4. **Connections over Isolation**: Always link texts, themes, ideas - this is critical for C6

5. **Quality over Quantity**: One well-analyzed poem beats three superficially read poems

6. **Feedback is Gold**: Get teacher feedback on everything you can, especially Part 2

7. **Track Everything**: Use Progress Tracker religiously - what gets measured gets done

8. **Review Regularly**: Spaced repetition is scientifically proven to work

9. **Balance All Competencies**: Don't just do theme analysis - cover all 6 competencies for each text

10. **Trust the Process**: You have 18 months. Follow this system and you'll be incredibly prepared.

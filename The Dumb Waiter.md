The Theatre of the absurd - after the second world war people lost their faith in humanity because of slaughter, mass killing and destruction, they witnessed.

People's lives became purposeless, meaningless and faithless.

Features:

- Silence
- Repetition
- Meaningless dialogues
- Not much of a plot
- Waiting for the arrival of something or somebody

Characters

Wilson - no face/No voice - makes him more menacing, dangerous, and threatening.
Ben - Hired Killer/Senior partner
Gus - Hired killer

- **Setting and Atmosphere:** The play is set in a **windowless, "dreary" basement room in Birmingham**. The environment is run-down and ill-equipped, immediately establishing a claustrophobic and menacing atmosphere.

- **Establishment of Status:** The initial stage directions highlight a **clear power imbalance** between the two hitmen. Ben is positioned on the left bed reading a newspaper, while Gus sits on the right bed, struggling with the mundane task of tying his shoelaces.

- **Ritualistic Physical Business:** The opening is characterized by **prolonged silence and repetitive non-verbal actions**. Gus stops tying his shoes twice to shake his feet and remove flattened objects—first a matchbox and then a cigarette packet—from his footwear.

- **The Role of the Newspaper:** Ben uses his newspaper as a **symbol of dominance and refinement**. He intermittently lowers the paper to glare at Gus, as if waiting for his subordinate to "mess up" so he can reprimand him.

- **The Broken Lavatory:** A recurring detail is the **non-functioning lavatory**. When Gus first exits to use it, the audience hears the chain pulled twice, but it fails to flush. This symbolizes the stagnation and neglect of their current situation. The toilet is dysfunctional just like the human life.

- **Initial Dialogue (The Lorry Story):** Ben breaks the silence by sharing a disturbing news report about an **eighty-seven-year-old man who was crushed by a lorry** after trying to crawl under it to avoid traffic.

    - Ben reacts with disgust, stating the story is enough to make one "puke".
    - Gus expresses disbelief, repeatedly calling the incident "unbelievable" and **"incredible"**.

- **Cycles of Stagnation:** Following this brief exchange, Gus shakes his head and exits to the lavatory a second time. The sequence ends with the **lavatory chain being pulled once without flushing**, while Ben whistles at a new item in the paper, using it to block out Gus's presence and maintain the status quo.

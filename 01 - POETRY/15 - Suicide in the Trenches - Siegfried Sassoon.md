# Suicide in the Trenches
**By Siegfried Sassoon**

*[1886 - 1967 / Post Modern Period]*

---

## Note on the Poet

Siegfried Loraine Sassoon was born on 8 September 1886 in Matfield, Kent, UK. His father was College and then Clare College, Cambridge University though he left without completing his a wealthy Jewish businessman and his mother an Anglo-Catholic. He was educated at Marlborough degree.

At the outbreak of first world war Siegfried, like many men of his generation, was full of patriotic sentiments and enthusiastic about the war effort. He joined the army just before war was declared. and needed medical treatment. In 1917, Sassoon was hospitalized. It was in hospital in Edinburgh, but broke his arm badly keeping him out of action until 1915. He also suffered from shell-shock that Sassoon became acquainted with fellow war poet, Wilfred Owen. They became very close, sharing a passion for war poetry and also a deep personal connection. Owen looked up to Sassoon as a mentor and guide: Sassoon looked over Owen's poetry and offered suggestions. He also met the war poets Rupert Brooke, and Robert Graves and had their influence.

Despite his decorations and reputation, in 1917 Sassoon decided to oppose the conduct of the war. One of the reasons for his violent anti-war feeling was the death of his friend David Cuthbert Thomas and it was very difficult to overcome his grief. At the end of a spell of convalescent leave, Sassoon declined to return to duty. He sent a letter to his commanding officer entitled Finished with the War: A Soldier's Declaration.

Sassoon married four times and had four children. (A daughter died, of a drug overdose, in 2002.) Siegfried Sassoon had many affairs with men, and a late marriage that produced his only son.

Counter-Attack and Other Poems can be considered Sassoon's best war poems, all of which are "harshly realistic laments or satires,". The later collection The War Poems of Siegfried Sassoon included 64 poems of the war, most written while Sassoon was in hospital recovering from his injuries. Public had different reactions to Sassoon's poetry. Some readers complained that the poet displayed little patriotism and some others admired them as realistic depiction of war. Sassoon expressed the feeling of trench warfare and the weariness of British soldiers for a war that seemed never to end. However, Sassoon protested the continuation of World War L

After the war, Sassoon became involved in Labour Party politics, lectured on pacifism, and continued to write. His most successful works of this period were his trilogy of autobiographical novels, The Memoirs of George Sherston. Some critics hold the view that Sassoon's best work is his prose, particularly the first two Sherston novels

In 1957 Sassoon became a convert to Catholicism and his spiritual concerns had been the predominant subject of his writing. These later religious poems are usually considered inferior to those written between 1917 and 1920.

He died from stomach cancer on 1 September 1967, one week before his 81st birthday. He is buried at St Andrew's Church, Mells, Somerset, not far from the grave of Father Ronald Knox whom he so admired.

## Analysis of the Stanzas

### Stanza - 1

> I knew a simple soldier boy  
> Who grinned at life in empty joy,  
> Slept soundly through the lonesome dark,  
> And whistled early with the lark.

At the beginning of the first stanza, the speaker introduces us a simple, happy young soldier boy whom he knew. The use of the alliteration "simple soldier boy" portrays the innocence of the boy and his lack of understanding on the concept of war. Moreover, the metaphor "grinned at life in empty joy" also gives the image of an immature and an inexperienced boy who turns into a soldier. On the other hand, the phrases like 'empty joy' looks rather paradoxical. The boy sleeps soundly even 'through the lonesome dark". Again, the boy seems at peace with himself and untroubled by any worries. Nevertheless, it is also ironic that 'lonesome' is not associated with happiness.

In the last line of this stanza, a lark is a bird known for its singing. The boy's whistling in the early morning with this bird metaphorically shows that he was happy and carefree. The spring bird lark, further symbolizes vitality of youth and the boy's early awakening suggests his enthusiastic and dynamic behavior.

Thus, superficially the boy seems to be in fine spirit of happiness, peacefulness and vitality. Nevertheless, there is an undercurrent pessimism expressed through the words like 'empty" 'lonesome' and 'dark".

### Stanza - 2

> In winter trenches, cowed and glum,  
> With crumps and lice and lack of rum,  
> He put a bullet through his brain.  
> No one spoke of him again

In the second stanza, the poet brings out a contrast with the first stanza. Accordingly, the poet chooses winter which symbolizes harshness and coldness. The soldier boy is no more grinning but, feels cowed and glum in winter trenches when fighting in the war on front lines. In the second line the word 'crumps' brings out the auditory image related to the sound of guns and bombs. 'Lice' suggests the dirtiness and the diseases spread in the trenches.

The lack of rum' suggests how the soldiers had to fight in winter without any motivation and intoxication to get rid of extreme cold. The third line gives the news that the young soldier commits suicide by putting 'a bullet through his brain'.

Perhaps, the young soldier would have been unable to bear up the harsh conditions of the battle field due to physical and mental immaturity. The last line conveys the mechanical and emotionless responses of the others. The death of a solder is losing only one number of the battle field and soldiers are just like cattle killed in a slaughter house as Wilfred Owen says in his poem 'Anthem For Doomed Youth"

### Stanza - 3

> You smug-faced crowds with kindling eye  
> Who cheer when soldier lads march by,  
> Sneak home and pray you'll never know  
> The hell where youth and laughter go.

In this stanza, the poet addresses the people who love and glorify war in an accusatory tone of 'you'. 'Kindling' is associated with fire and war is very destructive and spreading out of control just like fire. In the same way. 'smug faced crowds' who appear to be patriotic encourage and persuade young men to enlist war like adding fuel to fire. On the contrary, the poet is cursing and accusing such crowds that cheer young men who go to war, not knowing that they are sending them towards death and destruction. It is easy to kindle patriotic, glorious and positive feelings in young mind like 'Dulce et Decorum Est pro patria mori [It is sweet and proper to die for one's country']

The poet calls the soldiers 'soldier lads' because they are still not men who are mature enough to understand the true nature of war. The poet asks patriotic people to go home and pray. They never know the hell where young soldiers' youth and laughter go. The poet thereby, shows the real side of war and compares the battlefield with 'hell', a place where one is sent to be punished for sins.

Thus, the line "The hell where youth and laughter go" brings out the poet's negative attitude towards war. His view of war as an evil hell and it draws a contrast with the public patriotic view of war as a glorious place.

## Themes

'Suicide in the Trenches' is one of the poems written by the English poet Siegfried Sassoon in response to the horrors of the first world war. There are war poets who are in favour of war as well as against war. It is quite obvious that Siegfried Sassoon stands among the poets who have anti-war sentiments. He tries his best to give a realistic picture about war without being a blind patriot. Specially, this poem exposes and explores the pathetic condition of the trenches where the soldiers had to fight without conducive, hygienic health condition and basic facilities.

> In winter trenches, cowed and glum,  
> With crumps and lice and lack of rum,

The trenches filled with lice suggests how the young soldiers had to fight in a very loathsome environment and 'lack of rum' suggests that soldiers had nothing to forget their mental stress and physical weariness and get rid of cold weather condition. Perhaps, the high rank officers who wield the war machine and routine would have enjoyed the drinks of rum in much more safer and cozy office rooms.

The poet is also very critical about the war mongers and blood thirsty ambitious politicians who exploit the energy, vitality and heroism of youth to make them pawns of war game." No one spoke of him again" suggests that the soldiers who died in the war and sacrificed their young blood were forgotten and neglected without any honour and appreciation. The death of one soldier is just like one pawn on the chess board. Here, the poet exposes the hypocrisy and callousness of the war mongers and politicians who launch and justify war to fulfill their personal, commercial and global agendas.

Not only the political leaders but also general public cannot understand the true nature of war because they do not experience the bitterness and suffering of war as the young soldiers in the battle field. They are hypnotized and enchanted by the patriotic war slogans and glorious sentiments put forward by the war propaganda. The poet criticizes such people who convince and persuade young men to join war and fight for the country.

> "You smug-faced crowds with kindling eye  
> Who cheer when soldier lads march by,"

Siegfried further shows with a sarcastic tone how they think war is glorious by feeling proud of the young men fighting in the battle field.. But in reality, they can never imagine how they die and suffer in war. Therefore, the word kindling reveals the hypocrisy behind people who support war. They are blind to understand the consequences, sacrifice and price of this meaningless war. Millions of families lose their children because of the wrong concepts on war like 'Dulce et Decorum Est pro patria mori [It is sweet and proper to die for one's country']. As Wilfred Owen said "My subject is War, and the pity of War. The Poetry is in the pity" even Sassoon seems to follow his friend's statement on war in this poem.

In a nutshell, 'Suicide in the Trenches' mirrors Sassoon's protest against the pseudo patriotism, extreme militarism and the negligence of young war victims. In other words, he convinces that war is a curse and hell for the young.

## Poetic Techniques

### Form/Structure

There are 3 stanzas with 4 lines in each stanza. There is a methodical order of the ideas put forward in the three stanza respectively. The first stanza is the poet's observation of a soldier boy who feels happy, sleeps soundly and awakes in the morning with interest in adventures being unaware of the hostile world outside. The second stanza is totally different from the first stanza. The poet observes how the boy is suffering in the war trenches full of noise of the guns and bombs, unpleasantness of lice and lack of rum. The young soldier commits suicide being unable to bear up the depression that he has to undergo in such trenches. The third stanza is about the poet's anger and protest towards the responsible people who launch war and cheer the soldier lads to fight in war which becomes a hell for them.

### Rhyme Scheme / Meter

Rhyme scheme used in the poem is AABB, CCDD, EEFF and it reminds us of a nursery rhyme which is normally cheerful. Nursery rhyme and iambic tetrameter [8 syllables per line] make the poem lyrical.

> I knew a simple soldier boy - **A**  
> Who grinned at life in empty joy, - **A**  
> Slept soundly through the lonesome dark, - **B**  
> And whistled early with the lark. - **B**  
> In winter trenches, cowed and glum, - **C**  
> With crumps and lice and lack of rum, - **C**  
> He put a bullet through his brain. - **D**  
> No one spoke of him again. - **D**  
> You smug-faced crowds with kindling eye - **E**  
> Who cheer when soldier lads march by, - **E**  
> Sneak home and pray you'll never know - **F**  
> The hell where youth and laughter go. - **F**

### Narrative form

The point of view is third person. This is effective in showing one case of suicide, in third person observation, representing the depression one desire to die quickly.

### Language

Word choice [Diction] is simple. There is one long sentence in the first stanza. The young age of the soldier is stressed through the word 'boy' instead of using the word 'soldier' alone. Further the word 'simple suggests the boy's innocence and immaturity. "Empty joy" carries connotation of "lack of purpose'

As far as the Tense is concerned, the poem combines the simple past tense with the simple present tense. The simple past tense is used in the first two quatrains because they are about a young soldier whom the poet has a close acquaintance. The following verbs are used to describe the boy.  "grinned" (1. 2), "slept" (1. 3), "whistled" (1. 4), "put" (1. 7), "spoke" (1. 8). The poet describes a general situation using the simple present tense in the last quatrain. The verbs used in the quatrain are 'cheer', march, sneak, pray, know and go.

In the last stanza, the word 'kindling' is used to describe the eyes of the smug-faced crowds who feel happy to send young boys to the battle.

There is an oxymoronic and paradoxical way of using the words in 'Soldier boy' / 'empty joy/'soldier lads'

### Symbolism

The poem is about a young soldier boy's transformation from a happy and innocent person into a depressed soldier who desires to kill himself, because life is really worse than death.

The lark, which is a spring bird, symbolizes youth. The boy's awakening in the morning with the lark represents enthusiasm and his interest in adventures.

For the second stanza, the poet chooses winter season and it symbolizes the harshness and coldness of the war and death as well.

A contrast is drawn between the first stanza and the second stanza, when describing the young soldier. In the first stanza, 'grin' is replaced with "cowed and glum' It further implies that the young soldier's happiness vanishes and sense of horror emerges all around.

The hell symbolizes the evil of war and it is a contrast with the public's view of war as a glorious place. This contrast is drawn in order to convince the public that they are sending the young boys to hell being blind to the concepts of war like glory, dignity, patriotism and so on.

The 'lice' symbolizes dreadful hygienic condition spread in the trenches. 'Crumps' symbolize the destruction and violence caused by the use of destructive weapons and bombs. A drink of rum helped the soldiers to have a momentary relief by forgetting the dreadful images of war. The lack of rum ration provided to the soldiers fighting in the trenches increased the suicide rate among the soldiers causing depression.

### Irony

The tone seems to be in a disgrace tone that he is ashamed of the crowd cheering to the young soldiers that are going to war and going to die. It seems that the poet is against war and has a bad impression of it.

It is further ironic that the poet has used iambic tetrameter in the poem like in a nursery rhyme although it is about war and suicide.

### Tone

The tone in the poem is obviously bitter and sarcastic. In the last stanza. 'You smug-faced crowds with kindling eye' satirically suggests that general public is unable to empathize with young soldiers because they are hypnotized with patriotism, glory of war and so on. On the other hand they do not have personal experience of the bitterness and horror of war. Consequently, young soldiers untimely become victims of war.

### Sound Devices

In the first stanza 'Simple soldier' 'slept soundly', the repetition of the 's' produces a harsh hissing sound, and this contributes to the bitter tone throughout the poem. Also, the phrase 'slept soundly' expresses sleep soundly without worries and troubles before going to bed. This highlights how the boy was so innocent, without any worries at the start of his experience in war.

There is assonance in the line 'No one spoke of him again' repeating 'o' sound. It creates sympathy and empathy towards the young soldier who committed suicide and forgotten by others.

The word 'whistled' is onomatopoeic "And whistled early with the lark"

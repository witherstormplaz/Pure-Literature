# **Design By Robert Frost [1874-1963 / Modern Period]**

## **Note on the poet**

  

Robert Frost was an American poet, born on March 26, 1874, in San Francisco, California to journalist William Prescott Frost, Jr., and Isabelle Moodie. His mother was a Scottish immigrant. and his father descended from Nicholas Frost of Tiverton, Devon, England, who had sailed to New Hampshire in 1634 on the Wolfrana.

  

Frost's father was a teacher and later an editor of the San Francisco Evening Bulletin and an unsuccessful candidate for city tax collector Robert Frost's personal life was plagued with grief and loss. In 1885 when he was 11. his father died of tuberculosis, leaving the family with just eight dollars. After his death on May 5, 1885, the family moved across the country to Lawrence, Massachusetts, under the patronage of (Robert's grandfather) William Frost, Sr., who was an overseer at a New England mill. Frost graduated from Lawrence High School in 1892. Frost's mother joined the Swedenborgian church and had him baptized in it, but he left it as an adult.

  

Frost's mother died of cancer in 1900. In 1920, he had to admit his younger sister Jeanie to a mental hospital, where she died nine years later. Mental illness apparently ran in Frost's family, as both he and his mother suffered from depression.

  

Frost proposed to Elinor Miriam, who was attending St. Lawrence University, but she turned him down because she first wanted to finish school. Frost then decided to leave on a trip to Virginia, and when he returned, he proposed again. By then, Elinor had graduated from college, and she accepted. They married on December 19, 1895.

  

In 1894, he sold his first poem, "My Butterfly. An Elegy" (published in the November 8, 1894, edition of the New York Independent) for $15.

  

Frost attended Harvard University from 1897 to 1899. Frost's grandfather purchased a farm for Robert and Elinor in Derry, New Hampshire, Frost worked the farm for nine years while writing early in the mornings and producing many of the poems that would later become famous, Ultimately his farming proved unsuccessful and he returned to the field of education as an English teacher at New Hampshire's Pinkerton Academy from 1906 to 1911.

  

In 1912, Frost sailed with his family to Great Britain, settling first in Beaconsfield, a small town outside London. His first book of poetry, A Boy's Will, was published the next year. In England he made some important acquaintances, including T. E. Hulme, and Ezra Pound. Frost met or befriended many contemporary poets in England, especially after his first two poetry volumes were published in London in 1913 (A Boy's Will) and 1914 (North of Boston).

  

In 1915, during World War I, Frost returned to America, where Holt's American edition of A Boy's Will had recently been published, and bought a farm in Franconia, New Hampshire, where he launched a career of writing, teaching, and lecturing. This family homestead served as the Frosts' summer home until 1938. It is maintained today as The Frost Place, a museum and poetry conference site.

  

In 1924, he won the first of four Pulitzer Prizes for the book New Hampshire: A Poem with Notes and Grace Notes. He would win additional Pulitzers for Collected Poems in 1931. A Further Range in 1937, and A Witness Tree in 1943.[11]

  

For forty-two years from 1921 to 1963 Frost spent almost every summer and fall teaching Vermont. He is credited as a major influence upon the development of the school and its writing at the Bread Loaf School of English of Middlebury College, at its mountain campus at Ripton. programs

  

In 1960, Frost was awarded a United States Congressional Gold Medal. "In recognition of his poetry, which has enriched the culture of the United States and the philosophy of the world." ## **Note on Robert Frost's style of poetry**

  

Robert Frost is the poet who said "Poetry begins in delight and ends in wisdom" His poetry is deceptively simple, commonly using colloquial language as naturally as speech. Frost is a poet who seems to be simplistic in his writing styles, his rhyming schemes are surprisingly creative and innovative., often using iambic pentameter or blank verse. Frost is renowned for expressing themes related to nature. He usually points out modern man's relationship with nature. He metaphorically imitates the complex aspects of life in the 20th century. As readers, can initially appreciate the understated beauty of his cleverly used language, and are rewarded with beautiful imagery. However, when we consider the concepts which are explored in a poem by Robert Frost, we are then rewarded with knowledge, insight and, essentially; wisdom. When reading Frost's poetry, we are delighted by the exquisite use of imagery and sound devices like alliteration, consonance and assonance, and then rewarded with wisdom which is conveyed through metaphors.

  

However, readers are not the only ones who are delighted or gain wisdom; and we feel that as a poet, Robert Frost would have taken his wisdom and turned it into something more delightful through his poetry. Delight can be defined as something that gives great pleasure or enjoyment and this delight can come through the use of imagery, sound devices and colloquial language. Robert Frost's poetry is certainly delightful; however it was not intended to be purely aesthetic. The true meaning of Frost's poetry can only be uncovered once we take the time to consider the different layers of meaning. Whilst much of his poetry is elaborate and beautiful, it often alludes to concepts which are aphoristic.

  

## **Analysis of the lines Octave**

  

### **Lines 1-4**

  

I found a dimpled spider, fat and white,

On a white heal-all, holding up a moth

Like a white piece of rigid satin cloth

Assorted characters of death and blight

  

At the beginning of the poem, the poet describes us a scene he discovered from nature. The scene includes three organisms; a spider, a flower and a moth. The poet creates a visual image about the spider's physical appearance and colour as dimpled, fat and white in the first line.

  

In the second line, the poet creates another visual image of a beautiful little flower called a "heal-all. "Heal-all' flowers are usually blue in colour but here it is depicted as white. The name of the flower implies its healing power. It is believed that the flower has medicinal properties and belongs to the Mint family [Brunela vulgaris). Therefore, the ancient believed it as a panacea and called it Heal-all. Flowering plants of the Mint family typically have bluish flowers, not white ones. Once readers discover this, the heal-all becomes a peculiarity.

  

The fat and white spider holds up a moth on that pretty little healing flower. The flower has attracted the attention of a moth because it is drawn towards light by nature and therefore is more likely to move towards a white flower which radiates light even in dim conditions--than a blue flower. As a result, the moth has been killed by the white spider. A dress image is given to the moth to imply that it is like a white funeral shroud cut from a rigid satin cloth. As we know, satin cloth is silky and smooth. But it is ironic that the word 'rigid' is used to highlight its hard and inflexible quality. Perhaps the adjective 'rigid' may suggest that the moth is already dead on the flower due to the spider's venom.

  

However, the poet observes this design or pattern of whiteness and reflects upon it in this first part of the octave. This is like a scene of a drama that three assorted characters meet each other at one place. We wonder whether this meeting is a coincidence or a design of an invisible hand. All of these characters are associated with 'death' and 'blight'. Frost uses the words "death" and "blight," as obvious connotations of decay and degeneration Accordingly, the spider performs the main role as the evil predator and the moth is the innocent prey. The flower is the healer and the witness of this murder scene. Thus this natural scene is depicted with a little bit unnaturalness brought out by the white colour of the three characters. As readers we feel that there is something divine or devilish or mystic behind the incident.

  

We usually expect spiders to be black. So the whiteness of this spider brings us not only fun but also suspicion. Moreover, the white colour usually stands for something virtuous and pure, not evil or deadly. When considering the physical appearance of the spider, the adjectives like 'dimpled' and 'fat' give a cute look to the spider. In that ser sense, we cannot believe that such a lovely insect makes the innocent moth his prey on a white flower which symbolizes purity. On the other hand it is ironic that the flower which has healing power could not do anything to save the moth from the spider.

  

It is quite obvious that the poet deliberately conveys us that things are not always as they appear before our eyes. There may be illusions, deceptions, designs and so on in every action and reaction, incident and accident.

  

### **Lines 5-8**

  

Mixed ready to begin the morning right,

Like the ingredients of a witches' broth

A snow-drop spider, a flower like a froth,

And dead wings carried like a paper kite.

  

The white spider has already proved its evilness and immorality. Being white by appearance, it deceived the moth and made it his prey on the white petals of the flower. In the sixth line, the poet uses the simile "Like the ingredients of a witches' broth" and the word "mixed" to suggest that the spider, flower, and moth are "assorted characters" that have been selected and put together for the purposes of another being. They are ready to begin the morning by disclosing what happened at the previous night. The spider appears joyfully because of his tasty meal. The moth would lie lifeless after sucking the blood by the spider and the flower may look sick and shocked. It is obvious that there is no sense of a new beginning with the arrival of morning; instead there is a feeling that it will be tainted by the ugliness of this horrible occurrence.

  

Thus this scene proves that they have no power and control to go against their fate by themselves but must adhere to a mighty power's plan.

  

The simile "like the ingredients of a witches' broth". is also a literary allusion to the witches' scene of William Shakespeare's play Macbeth. The witches' broth contained the ingredients of animals, humans and plants such as newt's eye, frog's tongue, lizard's leg, finger of a baby, a Turk's nose and a root of hemlock. Some of these ingredients are innocent and harmless, but when they are mixed together, they become destructive and poisonous. In the same way, the various characters in the pattern witnessed by the poet are innocent and harmless when they are taken separately. Nevertheless, when they get together, the pattern becomes destructive and tragic.

  

On the other hand, it is quite evident that the witches are agents of evil in nature and their design is to make everything topsy-turvy. The witches' scene also convinces us that our lives may be controlled and manipulated and we may not have the power over our future. In that sense, this simile is appropriate to the scene depicted in the poem.

  

In the seventh line of the octave, it is quite ironic that the spider is called 'A snow-drop spider'. Again this metaphor enhances the whiteness and cuteness of the spider comparing it to the winter flower, snow-drop by camouflaging its true evilness. Moreover, the flower is likened to froth and the dead moth to a paper kite. Snow-drops symbolize nature's beauty, but in this case that lovable picture is ruined due to the murder committed by the spider. It is true that froth is delicate and fluffy. but it could be even frothing from mouth when poisoned.

  

In the line "And dead wings carried like a paper kite", the image of innocence and child playfulness is created using a simile to compare the dead moth's wings to something light as a kite. This comparison implies that the moth is weak by nature and it cannot prevent being a prey of the spider's strong legs. The "white heal-all" flower adds to this scheme of design as it helps to lure the innocent moth into its doom.

  

## **Sestet**

  

### **Lines 9-12**

  

What had that flower to do with being white,

The wayside blue and innocent heal-all?

What brought the kindred spider to that height,

Then steered the white moth thither in the night?

  

The beginning of the sestet marks the volta in the sonnet form according to traditional Petrarchan sonnets. It is also called 'turn' because there is a turn of the thought deviating from the octave. It is evident that even Frost follows the Petrarchan style marking a turn in the ninth line. The poet thereby, moves from observation to reflection. Frost wonders and questions what caused the naturally blue flower turned to white and why the moth and the spider came together on the flower at the same time. Furthermore, the spider could be seen harmless as its colour was pure white colour. The flower heal-all is innocent and it did not turn white itself. Even the name of the flower implies remedial and harmless. The spider did not force the moth to come. It came willingly with the attraction of the flower. Also, how did the spider predict the moth would fly there unless it was designed ?. Then the question arises whether there was anyone or anything created this unnatural scene.

  

The poet further asks what brought these characters together. The verbs 'brought" and "steered imply that some outside force must be responsible for bringing these assorted organisms in the night together to create the tragedy. It is ironic that the word 'kindred' is used to show the relationship between this spider and the flower in this scene. It is true to a certain extent as they have the same complexion 'white'. In that sense, the spider is considered pure and pious. Then, the question 'Who brought the kindred spider to that height' insinuates who brought such a sublime creature to an inhospitable, deplorable and unworthy level by tempting to take the innocent moth as a prey.

  

Thus, these questions asked by the poet himself develop an idea that some invisible force drove this coincidence into being and suggest it is an evil force.

  

### **Lines 13-14**

  

What but design of darkness to appall?

If design govern in a thing so small.

  

In Petrarchan sonnets, there is a turn in the ninth line and then a resolution in the couplet. But there is no definite resolution in Frost's couplet. Instead, there is another turn at the end and the rhyming couplet is presented according to the Shakespearean sonnet form. Moreover, instead of answers in the couplet, there is a question to find out the answer. The poet seems confused and appalled due to the things happening. The poet questions whether the universe is governed by limitless evil rather than goodness. The last line suggests that if the designer could do such an evil on a small scale, he could have the power to execute evil on larger scale as well.

  

## **Analysis of the poem**

  

Through his poem, "Design," Frost examines whether the things happen in the world according to a design or coincidence. It is this dichotomy that creates the central tension of the poem. Almost all the literary devices are employed to express this tension. Frost has a doubt whether all the deeds are controlled and designed by a cruel being. He asks several questions in the poem. His intention is to make his readers more critical and inquisitive on finding out the answers. He himself does not provide the answers. Instead, he sets up a multi-faceted argument about life which his readers have to resolve, if they can, for themselves.

  

In the first three lines of the poem, Frost tells his readers a story about a white spider sitting on a white flower. A white moth is caught by the spider on the white flower. The most extra-ordinary fact is that all these three figures are white. The scene becomes much more unusual when readers pay their attention to the denotation of "heal-all." A heal-all is a flower which has herbal value. These flowering plants are a part of the Mint family and have bluish flowers, not white ones.. Here the white spider gets attracted to the white flower to use it as a camouflage for his predatory purpose. The flower also attracts the attention of a moth, which is by nature drawn toward light and therefore is more likely to move towards the white flower which radiates light even in dim conditions than a blue flower. Consequently, the moth is killed by the white spider. The final six lines of the poem are a series of questions asked by Frost to know whether the flower, the spider, and the moth united at the same place and time with the same colour, white because of a contrivance or a mere chance.

  

The poem suggests that some other entity or force has specifically created the flower, white heal-all, the white spider, and the white moth and brought them together for a special purpose. The poet's speculations are expressed through the imagery and diction employed in the poem. The simile "Like the ingredients of a witches' broth" and the word "mixed" are employed to suggest that the spider, the flower, and the moth are "assorted characters" that have been selected and put together for the purposes of another being. They have no power to go against this invisible mighty power and all of them become part and victim of the design of the mystic designer. Moreover the characteristics the three organisms are not same except the white colour of the complexion. So this white colour creates a doubt in readers' mind if the spider, the flower, and the moth were made by the same creator, and the tragic scene was designed by him. The words "brought" and "steered" prove the fact that the spider and the moth did not come together under their own will and power, Once again, it is evident that some other force placed them together.

  

The image of the spider, flower, and moth as "ingredients of a witches" broth" is terrifying and wicked since witches are generally associated with the devil. This simile makes the entire scene confused because we have dilemma whether the designer is the Christian God or another malicious being like Satan.

  

When finding the answers, we feel that evil exists everywhere, though we consider some places are much more safer. The best example is the white flower which had even healing power could not save the innocent moth On the other hand we have no freedom for our existence. We are just like the players who act out on the stage of life according to the whims and fancies of the playwright He decides whether the actor should live or die according to his script. In the poem, the designer decided who should be the predator and the prey and where they should meet and what should be the background etc.

  

On the other hand it is very difficult for the ordinary people to define the things happen in nature. In the poem Tyger, William Blake questions whether the tiger was created by the same God who created the lamb. Through the questions asked by Blake, we can conclude that tiger was not created by Satan but God himself. That is because there should be badness to identify goodness. There should be both evil and virtues to understand the difference. If everyone is like meek lamb, there is no balance in nature. In other words, there should be both X and Y for comparing and contrasting. If all the creatures are innocent moths, their innocence and breeding without control will be a problem for the existence of others in nature. Therefore, a predator like the spider is very important to create the balance of nature.

  

Thus, there should be a mighty power like God to make the balance of nature to make the world a better place for all the living beings and non- living things. Thus, God designs everything good or bad for a worthy purpose. God's actions may look like set plans or coincidences before our eyes. Whatever the appearance is, there is a meaning of each and every action.. Furthermore, such divine actions may look mysterious or absurd like the scene in the poem superficially. But when analyzing deeply only, we can find out the reality. God makes us learn from all such incidents and accidents.

  

Thus Frost's design of the poem 'Design' lets us examine the true nature of nature, the existence of God and justification of God.

## **Literary Devices**

  

### **Form and Structure**

  

The poem 'Design' is a sonnet and it consists of the two parts, The Octave and the Sestet. The first eight lines belong to the Octave and the other six lines belong to the sestet. This type division is typical to Petrarchan sonnets. The beginning of the sestet usually [9th line] marks the volta in the Petrarchan sonnet form. It is also called 'turn' because there is a turn of the thought deviating from the octave. That is moving from observation to reflection.

  

In Petrarchan sonnets, there is a resolution in the end.. But in 'Design' there is no such resolution in the couplet. Instead, there is another turn at the end with a question raised by the poet to find out the answers. Moreover, the rhyming couplet reflects Shakespearean sonnet form.

  

### **Rhyme Scheme**

  

Design is a typical Petrarchan (or Italian) sonnet, including an octave (the first eight lines) and a sestet (the final six lines). Nevertheless. Robert Frost's theme is not love. The sestet concludes with which is more common to the scheme in the octave is the Petrarchan abba /abba, the sestet is a rare [acaa /cc,] rhyme pattern that is unusually restrictive for a poem in English poetry. Since the structure of the poem departs from tradition, the reader may wonder about its appropriateness to the poem "Design"

  

Perhaps Frost is mocking, or at least questioning, the very notion of order. On the other hand the disorder or the unusual arrangement of the rhyme scheme as well as the two turns in the sonnet suggest the complexity of the views put forward by him.

### **Language**

  

There are several adjectives used in the poem to describe the nouns. The spider is introduced as "dimpled spider" with another two adjectives fat and white to imply the cute and innocent look of the spider in an ironic or mystic manner. 'White' is the recurrent adjective used in the poem and it is mentioned five times in the poem because of its close association with the themes brought out in the poem. The other adjective phrases are as follows:

  

- rigid satin cloth
- assorted charters
- dead wings, white moth
- innocent heal-all etc.

  

### **Inversion**

  

Inversion is another technique which is related to the language of the poem. In the first line, 'found a dimpled spider, fat and white' the poet uses the technique of inversion where the normal word-order is inverted: By giving the word "white" at the end, he highlights the whiteness of the spider and the motif of whiteness that runs throughout the poem.

  

### **Irony**

  

It is believed that "heal-all' flower has healing power because of its medicinal property. This flower is holding up the moth when the spider takes it as his prey. It is ironic that the flower could not heal the moth which died of spider's venom. It is further ironic that the ugly venomous spider is called as 'snow-drop spider" Here, snowdrop may be the flower that blooms in late winter season or just a drop of snow to imply the white colour and innocence of that particular spider. In the 11th line of the sestet, the phrase "kindred spider" is also ironic because the evil poisonous spider is depicted as a relation to the innocent flower.

  

### **Simile**

  

In the third line the moth is likened to a piece of cloth and also in the eighth line, a paper kite. Spider moth and flower are seen as the three ingredients of a witches' broth in the play Macbeth.

  

### **Metaphor**

  

Image of this white spider on a "white heal-all" is used as a metaphor for the evil that is tarnishin the world that is supposedly created in God's image. That is to say, Frost uses the "white heal-a as a metaphor for the world as created in God's image, the moth being included in this supposes innate innocence, while the spider represents the malevolence hidden within it.

  

### **Symbolism**

  

'White' is highly significant in this poem because it is mentioned several times. It is a symbol of purity and innocence and is often associated with angelic beings in the Bible.

  

### **Imagery**

  

The most striking image is the design or the pattern of whiteness of the scene observed by the poet Accordingly, the spider holds in its mouth a white moth, white like a piece of rigid satin cloth Thus the white spider, the white flower, and the white moth with dead wings. "carried like a paper kite" build up the visual image of the design or pattern.

  

### **Biblical and literary allusions**

  

In Shakespeare's Macbeth the three witches appear in the first scene, and taunt the leading protagonist Macbeth with their evil chanting of bubble, bubble toil and trouble. They eventually forecast Macbeth's downfall, implying that there is some grand design. The broth made from strange ingredients represents the dark sides of the witches and how they control and manipulate living ones and non living things of nature. This literary allusion is very appropriate to the scene of the poem to discover the forces behind the design.

  

The most significant association to the Bible is the use of the word thither, which means to or towards a certain place. It appears most notably in John 7:34, when Christ says 'And where I am, thither ye cannot come.

  

### **Repetition**

  

Repetition is another technique we find in this poem. The repetition of "white" for example creates a 'death-like' effect that pervades the poem.

  

### **Ambiguity**

  

Ambiguity is another technique used in the end of the poem where we are left wondering whether the whole thing is a mere coincidence or a "design" of some unseen power. Even the rhetorical questions in the sestet are very much effective to create ambiguity.

  

### **Oxymoron**

  

The phrases like "dimpled spider, fat and white" / "A snowdrop spider" / "rigid satin" can be taken as oxymoronic figures.

  

### **Enjambment**

  

The octave is in fact one long sentence broken up into various clauses by astute use of punctuation like dashes and commas without losing meaning.

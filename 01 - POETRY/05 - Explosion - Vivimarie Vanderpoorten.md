# **Explosion** **By Vivimarie Vanderpoorten**

## **Note on the poet**

Vivimarie Vanderpoorten Vivimarie VanderPoorten is a Sri Lankan poet. She was born in Kandy and grew up in Kurunegala. She taught poetry and Economics at the University of Kelaniya and completed her Masters in applied linguistics at the University of Ulster, Northern Ireland and is currently a senior lecturer in English language, literature and linguistics at the Open University of Sri Lanka.

Her book Nothing Prepares You won the 2007 Gratiaen Prize. She was also awarded the 2009 SAARC Poetry Award in Delhi. Explosion is a poem from her first collection 'Nothing Prepares You' She usually focuses on gender, race, class, identity, struggle, suffering, war, politics and love in her writing.

## **Background of the poem**

The poem Explosion is based on one of the deadliest attacks carried out in Colombo during the 30 year war in Sri Lanka. It was the bombing of the Central Bank on 31, January 1996 at around 10.45-11 a.m. The Central Bank of Sri Lanka, the central nerve system of the country's economy. was paralyzed by a massive bomb explosion launched by the LTTE. The Central Bank attack has entered the annals of the history books as one of the most brutal deed committed during the war.

According to the reports, the truck (42-6452) containing about 440 pounds of high explosives had crashed through the main gate of the Central Bank. As gunmen traded fire with security guards, the suicide bomber in the lorry detonated the massive bomb, which tore through the bank and damaged eight other buildings nearby.

The lorry was followed by a three-wheeler, carrying two LITE cadres armed with automatic rifles and an RPG launcher. Among the wounded were two US citizens, six Japanese, and one Dutch national. Most of them were bystanders or civilians manning small shops set up near the bank. The bomber was identified as Raju, and died immediately. His backup team consisted of Subramanium Vigneswaram and Sivasamy Dharmendra and they were apprehended by law enforcement with information provided by the public. Police and the security forces launched a massive manhunt for others who were involved in this terrorist incident. It was found that the bombers had come from Jaffna, in the north of the country and were LTTE members.

## **Analysis of the stanzas**

### **Stanza -1**

On the day the truckload

of explosives

drove into the central bank,

for a long second

time staggered

All sounds of a workday morning

in the city

even the cawing of the crows

merged into a solitary

Boom

Prism of fire and fury

In the first stanza, the poetess reminds us the Central Bank bombing which was one of the deadliest terrorist attacks carried out by the LTTE on January 31, 1996, during the separatist civil war in Sri Lanka. Accordingly, the truck containing high explosives drove into the central bank. The poem gives a vivid description of the moment of the bomb explosion maintaining a slow space how time stood almost still. Moreover, all the sounds heard of the work day morning in the busy Colombo city including even the crows' habitual cawing merged into a single loud sound of the bomb explosion converting the city to a prism of fire and fury. Thus the poetess juxtaposes all the visual and auditory imagery to express the horror created by the bomb blast.

### **Stanza - 2**

Lives ended

eyes were blinded

retired wage earners

collecting provident funds

were crushed under brick and glass

the nearby vegetable seller's

hands were severed

like cucumbers,

Women in sari

held their eyeballs in their palms

and blood spattered

the streets,

erasing memory.

This stanza brings out a series of scenes of the devastation of the bomb blasting very vividly. There were a lot of victims either being dead or seriously injured. There were many dead bodies scattered. Some were blinded. It is pathetic that the pensioners who came to collect provident funds were crushed under the rubble of the fallen and broken bricks and glasses. A nearby vegetable seller's hands were severed and mutilated. It is a very poignant and a shocking scene that the eye-balls of some women in sari gorged out and held in their palms due to the explosion. Most probably, they could be the employees of the central bank. The streets were covered with blood and some were in traumatic shock with oblivion. Thus it is quite obvious that most of the civilians became the victims of the terrorist bomb blast.

Stanza - 3

Out of the broken window

of a damaged car -

dead driver -

the radio blared, unscathed

on a commercial break

a man's pleasant voice

announced

that big or small, insurance

protects them all

The third stanza depicts a damaged car with its dead driver inside and how a loud commercial aired out of the broken window of the car over the undamaged. It is quite ironic that the announcer with his pleasant voice announced the commercial jingle for an insurance company "big or small, insurance/protects them all". The poet is critical about the cheerful slogan wrapped with promises, protection and coverage because the insurance companies could not ensure the protection of the people who became victims of this bomb explosion. They are just like catching fish in the troubled water because they can easily tempt people for their insurance policies showing the possibility of being victims of such unexpected violence and bomb explosions

### **Themes of the poem**

The poem "Explosion' deals with the central bank bombing which was carried out by the LTTE on January 31st, 1996, in the city of Colombo, which killed nearly 91 and injured thousands. It was one of the most horrible terrorist attacks during the 30 year war. According to the reports, a truck, containing explosives crashed through the main gate of the Central Bank, and immediately a suicide bomber detonated the bomb. It destroyed the bank and damaged the buildings nearby. The central bank is the monetary authority of Sri Lanka, and the apex institution in the country's financial sector. The bank is also responsible for the conduct of monetary policy in Sri Lanka and also has wide supervisory powers over the financial system. If a nation's economy were a human body, then its heart would be the central bank. And just as the heart works to pump life-giving blood throughout the body, the central bank pumps money into the economy to keep it healthy and growing. Then what was the ulterior motive of the LTTE terrorists to attack the central bank? It is quite obvious that they wanted to paralyze and jeopardize the entire economy of Sri Lanka.

The poetess exposes the inhumanity and callousness of terrorism through this attack. They are insensitive to the loss of human lives when they try to accomplish their target. Consequently, a lot of civilians who have no any connection with politics or atrocity with terrorists became victims on that fateful day.

As the poet points out, especially the old people or the senior citizens who came to collect their provident funds to spend the rest of their life very happily and peacefully had to rest in peace unexpectedly. Therefore, terrorists do not mind whether their victims are old or young when creating chaos in the country. The vegetable seller who was seriously injured severing the hands represents the thousands of poor people who become disable being unable to continue their daily scanty earning. They have neither pensions nor insurance benefits to continue their life. The poet thereby, points out how the poor people suffer due to such bomb blasts as terrorists do not consider whether their victims are poor or rich. The women in sarees could be the employees of the central bank and they would have come to report their duty as usual on the day of the bomb explosion. Again it proves the fact that terrorists are callous to kill even the duty-conscious government employees who contribute their knowledge and skills for the development of the country irrespective petty ethnic disparities. Thus, it is quite evident that terrorism is a curse for the people of all walks of life.

The poetess not only conveys the themes associated with terrorists acts but also highlights the profits earned by businessman during war situations. The slogan of the radio advertisement of the insurance company implies how the insurance companies introduce various types of insurance policies so as to exploit people who are exposed to vulnerability and insecurity due to terrorist acts. Thus, war is commercialized to maximize the profits by promoting and propagating their business In other words when war becomes a curse for the war victims, it is a boon for the business class in insurance companies as well as the other enterprises.

## **Poetic Techniques**

### **Form**

There are three stanzas in the poem written in free verse form. There is no regular number of lines in each stanza or uniformity of the length of lines. This free verse form is appropriate to reflect the chaos and mayhem caused by the bomb explosion.

### **Narrative form**

The poem is presented from the third person narrative form. The poet appears as an eye witness of the scene and reports what she observed and witnessed in a more realistic and authentic manner. She does not make any comment on the destruction. Instead, she lets readers comment and present their views.

### **Language / Diction**

The simple past tense is used to reminisce the scenes of the fateful day of the bomb blast through the memory flashback technique

**Examples:**

"On the day the truckload of explosives drove into the central bank. for a long second

"Women in sari held their eyeballs in their palms and blood spattered"

"on a commercial break a man's pleasant voice announced"

- The choice of words is simple and direct so that it is easy for him to present a very vivid picture of the entire destruction.
- There are lines with both long syllables and short syllables. The long syllables suggest the slow face, secretive approach of the truckload of the explosives, scattered unattended dead bodies, continuous slogan of the advertisement of the insurance company etc.

**Examples:**

"On the day the truckload of explosives"

"Women in sari held their eyeballs in their pams and blood spattered"

"the radio blared, unscathed on a commercial break"

- The short syllables and short lines articulate the immediacy or the abruptness of the things happened at the moment of the bomb blast.

**Examples:**

"Prism of fire and fury"

"Lives ended"

"were crushed under brick and glass"

### **Personification:**

**Examples:**

"On the day the truckload of explosives drove into the central bank,"

Here, "the truckload of explosives" is personified In the first stanza, the poetess personifies time as "time staggered" which, in effect, suggests the dragging of time compared to the suddenness of the explosion.

"for a long second time staggered"

## **Sound effects:**

### **Alliteration**

The alliteration of the "d" sound in the "dead driver" in the last stanza creates a feeling of morbidity in the reader's mind.

"of a damaged car -dead driver"

### **Assonance**

The assonance of the "o" sound in the first 4 lines of the first stanza creates a sense of passivity just before the terrible explosion.

"On the day the truckload af explosives drove into the central bank, for a long second"

### **Onomatopoeia**

The poetess uses onomatopoeia in the first stanza in a very effective manner. For example, the innocent "cawing of the crows" contrast sharply with the shocking "boom" of the terrible explosion.

### **Simile**

The poetess employs a simile in the lines "hands were severed/like cucumbers," which suggests the tragic impact of the bomb on the innocent human lives

### **Imagery**

The use of powerful imagery is the most outstanding quality of the poem. For example, "the prism of fire and fury" at the end of the first stanza creates a vivid image of the explosion. Also the gruesome image of the women holding their eyeballs in their palms suggests brutality of the terrorism., especially with regard to the innocent civilians. The "blood spattered" streets is another sharp visual image of the bomb attack

### **Irony**

The last two lines of the poem "that big or small, insurance/protects them all" brings out sharp irony regarding the insurance companies that come forward to protect people only after the vast havoc. Insurance companies can identify the material losses under their covering policies. Nevertheless, they cannot recover the lost lives.

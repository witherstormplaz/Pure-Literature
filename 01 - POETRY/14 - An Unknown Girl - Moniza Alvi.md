# An Unknown Girl
**By Moniza Alvi**

*[1954 - / Contemporary Period]*

---

## Note on the Poet

Moniza Alvi is a Pakistani-British poet and writer. She was born in Lahore, Pakistan, to a Pakistani father and a British mother in 1954. Her father moved to Hatfield, Hertfordshire, in England when Alvi was a few months old. She did not re-visit Pakistan until after the publication of one of her first books of poems — *The Country at My Shoulder*. She worked for several years as a high-school teacher, but is now a freelance writer and tutor, living in Norfolk. She and her husband, Robert, have one daughter.

Alvi says: "Presents from my Aunts in Pakistan was one of the first poems I wrote. When I wrote this poem I wasn't actually back in Pakistan. The girl in the poem would be me at about 13. The things aren't straightforward for her. I found it was important to write the Pakistan poems because clothes seem to stick to her in an uncomfortable way, a bit like a kind of false skin, and she thinks she is getting in touch with her background. And maybe there's a bit of a message behind the poems about something I went through, that I want to maybe open a few doors if possible."

*Peacock Luggage*, a book of poems by Moniza Alvi and Peter Daniels, was published as a result of the two poets winning together the Poetry Business Prize in 1991, in Alvi's case for "Presents from my Aunts in Pakistan." That poem and "An Unknown Girl" have featured on England's GCSE exam syllabus for young teenagers.

Since then, Moniza Alvi has written four poetry collections. *The Country at My Shoulder* (1993) led to her being selected for the Poetry Society's New Generation Poets promotion in 1994. She also published a series of short stories, *How the Stone Found its Voice* (2005), inspired by Kipling's *Just So Stories*.

In 2002 she received a Cholmondeley Award for her poetry. In 2003 a selection of her poetry was published in a bilingual Dutch and English edition. A selection from her earlier books, *Split World: Poems 1990-2005*, was published in 2008.

On 16 January 2014, Alvi participated in the BBC Radio 3 series "The Essay - Letters to a Young Poet." Taking Rainer Maria Rilke's classic text, *Letters to a Young Poet* as their inspiration, leading poets wrote a letter to a protégé.

## Note on the Poem

"An Unknown Girl" is a semi-autobiographical poem composed by Moniza Alvi. Therefore, it is evident that the poem mirrors the identity of the writer herself. She always had an inner struggle with her cultural identity. Once she said: "I never feel entirely at home in England and of course I'm not a part of the Asian community at all." This mentality reflects in most of her poems.

According to this poem, the persona visits a bazaar in India where she gets her hands decorated with henna tattoos of a peacock. Hennaing is a form of body art in India and some other Asian countries using plant dyes of henna.

The poem is about the persona's responses and feelings at the moment of applying henna on her hand by an Indian girl for the design of the tattoo.

## Analysis of the Poem

### Lines 1-9

> In the evening bazaar  
> Studded with neon  
> An unknown girl  
> Is hennaing my hand  
> She squeezes a wet brown line  
> From a nozzle  
>
> She is icing my hand,  
> Which she steadies with her  
> On her satin peach knee

According to these lines, it is quite obvious that it is the evening and the scene is set in a bazaar [market]. The neon signs are the main source of lighting and they create a visual and colour image to bring out a modern and western type of atmosphere all around. At this moment, the persona seems to be sitting in a stall at the market place getting her hand decorated with henna by an unknown girl. The expression "unknown" adds a mysterious quality to the girl from the point of view of the persona.

Henna is a tropical shrub and hennaing is a traditional Indian and Pakistani art. The verb "squeezes" is onomatopoeic, and creates a visual and colour image when extracting the dye from a tube. Anointing the wet dye to the persona's hand is a sensual action and she feels the cool effect of this smooth conditioner. The word "icing" has two meanings. One of them is the formation of ice on a surface. In that sense the persona feels her hands being iced. The other meaning is the sweet coating used to decorate cakes. In that sense, the poetess draws a contrast through the two words since "hennaing" is associated with the Indian or eastern culture whereas "icing" is related to the west.

The persona who is familiar with the western culture at present may tend to define and feel the unknown girl's action of decorating her hand as icing. Thus the word "icing" is very significant as a tactile image as well as a metaphor for the unknown girl's action. The two persons involved in this scene are women and usually women are interested in these henna tattoos so that it reflects the feminine aspects of the culture.

The word "steadies" suggests how the persona feels some comfort and stability from the experience of keeping her hands on the unknown girl's nicely shaped knees covered with smooth silky fabrics. The persona's keen observation and close attention to the unknown girl's physical beauty suggests her awakening attraction to the Indian and Pakistani culture.

### Lines 10-17

> In the evening bazaar  
> For a few rupees —  
> An unknown girl  
> Is hennaing my hand  
> As a little air catches  
> My shadow stitched kameez  
> A peacock spreads its lines  
> Across my palm.

Again the lines "In the evening bazaar," "An unknown girl is hennaing my hand" appeared at the beginning of the poem are repeated to emphasize the time and place. The girl does this art work only for a few rupees. It suggests her humbleness and hospitality towards the persona. She does not have any hidden purpose to exploit the foreigner through her competence on the traditional art. On the other hand, the persona derives more intrinsic satisfaction, novel experience and a higher value from the henna tattoo which materially costs very little.

The persona could be wearing a traditional Indian dress. The phrase "My shadow-stitched kameez" creates a visual image to convey the longing of the persona to immerse herself in the Indian culture. Kameez is an Indian and Pakistani dress which symbolizes Indian culture. In metaphorical sense, the salwar-kameez worn by Indian women is a loose fitting dress just like her shadow, bigger than herself. When the persona says "my shadow stitched kameez," it also becomes a metaphor for her identity influenced by Indian and Pakistani culture.

The last two lines in this extract suggest that the tattoo design on the persona's palm is a peacock and it is the national bird in India. Therefore, it signifies and intensifies the persona's enthusiasm to expose and embrace the Indian culture. Moreover, the expression "A peacock spreads its lines" indicates the unknown girl's creative ability of hennaing live and lovely images.

### Lines 18-27

> Colours leave the street  
> Float up in balloons.  
> Dummies in shop-fronts  
> Tilt and stare  
> With their western perms.  
> Banners for Miss India 1993  
> For curtain cloth  
> And sofa cloth  
> Canopy me. I have new brown veins.

There is another metaphor in the first two lines of this extract. The passing of time is depicted in this metaphor. Accordingly, it convinces us how gradually and eventually the darkening atmosphere grows late and the evening turns to night and colours fade away. The verb "float" suggests the theme of loss.

The traditional images of India created so far are contrasted with modern western images such as "Dummies in shop-fronts," "tilt and stare," "with their Western perms."

As time passes and colours fade away, the persona imagines that the mannequins in the shop windows are staring at her. They are personified as staring at her because she is losing her western identity once shared with them. She also feels that the people who follow the westerners are like the dummies displayed in the shops because they seem artificial and fake compared to her present surroundings.

The line "Banners for Miss India 1993" brings out a more modern image of India. "Miss World" is not an Indian concept. It was introduced by the westerners and it has also invaded the Indian culture and functions as an annual event of the country. Although western concepts are lurking here and there, the rest of the lines with the phrase "Canopy me" conveys how the persona is protected by the other dominant aspects of the Indian and Pakistani culture.

The last line of this extract "I have new brown veins" implies that the traditional Indian culture has become a part of her blood and body now. This really highlights her feeling of cultural identity with India.

### Lines 28-35

> In the evening bazaar  
> Very deftly  
> An unknown girl is hennaing my hand  
> I am clinging  
> To these firm peacock lines  
> Like people who cling to sides of a train.

At the beginning of this extract, the persona again repeats how the unknown girl is still hennaing her hand very skillfully. The persona's strong emotional attachment to Indian culture is expressed by the words "clinging" and "firm peacock lines." Moreover, the persona's clinging to the firm peacock lines is compared to the Indians' travelling by train clinging to the sides of a train despite the risks. Train is a common mode of transport in India and overcrowded trains are also common sights in the country. This visual image and simile also signify the socio-economic aspects of India and Pakistan.

### Lines 36-43

> Now the furious streets  
> Are hushed. I'll scrape off  
> The dry brown lines  
> Before I sleep,  
> Reveal soft as a snail trail  
> The amber bird beneath.  
> It will fade in a week,

According to the first two lines, the persona observes that the busy and noisy streets are now quiet. She will brush away the brown lines before she sleeps. Once the dry brown lines are removed, the image of an "amber bird" emerges and it is soft as a snail trail. She describes the soft nature of the henna and scraping off the brown lines to reveal the beauty underneath. It is quite evident that she herself experiences a period of cultural metamorphosis or transition.

The line "It will fade away in a week" means that the surface image of the henna will fade in a week. It further refers to the circumstances that compel her to go back to England where she lives at present.

### Lines 44-48

> When India appears and reappears  
> I'll lean across a country  
> With my hands outstretched  
> Longing for the unknown girl  
> In the neon bazaar.

The persona feels that she has achieved a new identity. Although the henna design fades away in a week, India will appear and reappear in her haunting memory forever. She imagines how she outstretches her hands towards the unknown girl in the neon bazaar longing for henna tattoos. Thus the last lines suggest the persona's strong wish to have a close contact with the Indian subcontinent and her birth heritage in heart and soul.

## Themes of the Poem

"An Unknown Girl" reflects Moniza Alvi's attempt to search for her own cultural identity in a country to which she belongs but actually she is unable to call it her own motherland. Therefore, the theme of belonging and loss is conveyed very vividly throughout the poem.

As far as the setting of the poem is concerned, it is a market place in the evening with neon signs as the main source of lighting. The persona seems to sit in a stall, getting her hand decorated by henna tattoo by a mysterious "unknown" girl who works for a few rupees. At that moment, the persona imagines that the mannequins in the shop windows are staring at her. As the henna design is completed, she feels that she has brown veins because of the new cultural identity she has gained through this new experience. Nevertheless, when she removes the dried henna from her palm that night the design will fade away in a week.

It is quite evident that Alvi herself appears as the persona in the poem. Being born in Pakistan, she has rights and roots in Indian culture. Although she was born in Indian culture, she was bred in western culture. During her visit to India, she would have thought of searching for her true cultural identity whether it is western or eastern. The traditional Indian body art of hennaing takes her to a dreamy world and makes her think that she has "brown veins," belonging to Indian and Pakistani culture. But the western type of commercial items like dummies displayed in the shop fronts and the western concepts like the contests of Miss India disturb her new discovery of her own Indian identity. It is ironic that the new identity she found about herself is temporary just like the henna tattoo on her palm. She is an unknown girl among her known people in her native land because she has been living in her adopted country, England for many years.

The conflict faced by the persona or Alvi is common to many people in the world. It is obvious that they lose their cultural roots specially due to bi-cultural marriages and immigrations. Such people do not feel that they belong to one country or a culture so that they become culturally rootless and alienated forever in their lifetime.

### Main Themes

- Desire for discovering cultural identity
- Sense of belonging and loss
- Relationship with the familiar and the unknown
- Split cultural identity
- Hybrid culture
- Native country and adopted country

## Analysis of the Poem (Literary Analysis)

As far as the analysis of the poem is concerned, it is a semi-autobiographical work in which Moniza Alvi explores her dual heritage. The persona's visit to the evening bazaar becomes a symbolic journey of self-discovery. The hennaing ritual serves as a bridge between her western upbringing and her eastern roots. Through vivid imagery, metaphor, and simile, Alvi conveys the complexity of cultural identity and the transient nature of belonging.

---

## Literary Devices

### Form and Structure

Form and structure used in the poem is very important to reveal the cultural identity of the poetess. The short lines create a fast rhythm and they also reflect the busy and noisy atmosphere of the evening bazaar. On the other hand, a series of images are presented to the reader through the structure of the poem.

### Narrative Form

First person narrative form is used in the poem. It is very much appropriate because the poetess is able to share feelings, emotions and experience with the reader in a live and dramatic manner.

### Setting

Moniza Alvi was born in Pakistan. Her father was Pakistani and mother English. She left Pakistan when she was a baby. The poet is thus caught between two worlds and her poems exemplify her quest for her cultural identity. The prescribed poem appears to be set in India. Pakistan was a part of India before the partition, therefore the setting may be a symbolic thirst for her motherland.

### Language

As far as the choice of words is concerned, there is a contrast of the words selected by the poetess to signify Indian culture and western culture. For example the words like bazaar, hennaing, peacock, rupees, kameez etc., symbolize Indian culture and the words like neon, icing, banners of Miss India 1993, dummies etc. symbolize western culture.

Enjambment [run-on lines] implies the continuation of the actions like hennaing and the other activities in the bazaar.

## Poetic Techniques

### Metaphors / Symbols / Imagery

- "In the evening bazaar" >> symbol for an Indian market place.
- "Studded with neon" >> visual and colour image for modern and western type of atmosphere.
- "An unknown girl" >> metaphor and symbol for unfamiliar yet native culture.
- "Is hennaing my hand" >> symbol of Indian and Pakistani art.
- "She squeezes a wet brown line" >> metaphor and symbol for Indian culture.
- "She is icing my hand" >> symbol and metaphor for western culture.
- "On her satin peach knee" >> symbol and image for physical beauty.
- "For a few rupees" >> symbol of humbleness and hospitality / Indian identity.
- "My shadow stitched kameez" >> metaphor and symbol for Indian culture.
- "A peacock spreads its lines" >> symbol of Indian culture [National bird of India].
- "Colours leave the street" >> image of temporariness of the experience.
- "Dummies in shop-fronts" >> metaphor / visual image for western culture.
- "Banners for Miss India 1993" >> a symbol / metaphor for westernization / loss of Indian culture.
- "I have new brown veins" >> metaphor / colour image for the new experience of Indian culture.
- "Like people who cling to sides of a train" >> simile for the attachment to Indian culture / visual image for lack of transport facilities [Poverty in India].
- "Now the furious streets" >> symbol for busy and noisy streets in India.
- "I'll scrape off / The dry brown lines" >> visual image and symbol for leaving away the new identity.
- "Reveal soft as a snail trail" >> simile for slow transition from person's present western cultural identity to new Indian cultural identity.
- "The amber bird beneath" >> metaphor for the preciousness of Indian culture.
- "It will fade in a week" >> visual image for the temporary attachment to the new experience of Indian culture.
- "When India appears and reappears" >> visual image for the persona's haunting memory.

### Personification

The poetess personifies the peacock in the lines:

> "A peacock spreads its lines  
> Across my palm"

Peacock represents beauty and pride. It is also the national bird in India. The use of personification suggests that the bird is almost live on the persona's palm. It implies that the persona lives with her own root culture at that moment.

### Title of the Poem

The title of the poem is "An Unknown Girl" refers to a single girl in the evening bazaar. The word "unknown" implies mystery and uncertainty. Although it may refer to the girl in the poem, it is appropriate to the poetess herself as she is unknown to the roots, the unconscious culture and heritage ingrained in her. Therefore, it could imply the identity of the narrator which was "unknown" within her.


# **The Fisherman Mourned by his Wife By Patrick Fernando [1931-1982 / Sri Lankan Poet]**

## **Note on the poet**

  

Patrick Fernando is a Sri Lankan poet. He was born in 1931 to a Roman Catholic middle class family in Kalutara, on the south west coast of Sri Lanka. He was highly influenced by the colonial heritage of Si Lanka. He received higher education at the University of Ceylon and worked in the Income Tax Department until his death. Although he was a tax officer by profession, he loved literature very much. He had a sound knowledge about Western classical literature, Roman and Greek mythology. He was very much interested in Sociology and Theology as well. He was well-versed not only in Sinhalese and English but also in Greek and Latin.

  

He loved nature too. So he had a keen interest in bird watching and fish-rearing. Whenever he was free, he got involved in gardening. He loved large trees, foliage plants, anthuriums and orchids. He spent most of his weekends supervising his coconut plantation at Mangala Eliya. He also liked Western classical music.

  

Though he was a Sri Lankan poet, he could write as an Englishman. Therefore, he was well-recognized in Sri Lankan English poetry and he could earn a name and fame in world literature as well due to some universal themes conveyed through his writings. Although some of his poems had local colour, he employed very creative and innovative techniques.

  

He wrote many poems reflecting his vast knowledge of socio-economic, cultural, theological and classical aspects and many other spheres. His first collection 'The Return of Ulysses' and other poems were published in Britain. Another collection was published after his death by the Oxford University Press in New Delhi.

  

"Adam and Eve", "The Fisherman Mourned by His Wife", "For Paul Claudel", "A Symphony in Flowers", "The Lament of Paris", "Oedipus Solitary", "Aeneas and Dido", "The Decline of Aspasia", "Elegy for December", "Meditation over five Graves", "A Fallen Tree", "Life and Death of a Hawk", "Obsequies of the Late Antonio Pompirelli", "Oedipus: The Last Days", "One Flock, One Shepherd", "A Coat of Many Colors", "Pictures for a Chapel of the Passion", The way of the Adjutant Stork, The Magi, To Isabel, Father and Son and "Elegy for My Son" are some of the poems written by him.

  

Patrick Fernando felt totally broken when his youngest son died in 1980 and he could not endure the strain and shock for long and he died in Jan. 1982.

  

## **Analysis of stanzas**

  

### **Stanza -1**

  

When you were not quite thirty and the sun

had not yet tanned you into boat brown,

when you were not quite thirty and had not begun

to be embittered like the rest, nor grown

obsessed with death, then would you come

hot with continence upon the sea

chaste as a gull flying pointed home

in haste to be with me!

  

- tanned-skin became darker by exposure to the sun.
- embittered made someone bitter, sad. resentful.
- obsessed preoccupied or filled the mind continually with something.
- continence self-control.
- chaste abstaining from extra marital relationships.
- haste-hurry/quickness.

  

The speaker of the poem is the wife of a fisherman who is dead at this moment. The scene depicted in the poem is the wife's mourning in the form of a dramatic monologue. Accordingly, the wife reminisces how her husband faced such an untimely death at his immature age. She repeats the age of her husband 'not quite thirty' to emphasize his unexpected death which is unbearable for her. The second line implies that he is so new and inexperienced to the hard life of fishing. As a result, he had not been exposed to the hot sun-shine much to get the sun burnt tan colouerd skin when he died. She also remembers that her husband was not worried about the bitter and harsh nature of fishing as other fishermen. Moreover, he was not conscious and sensitive to the unpredictability and uncertainty of life being too much obsessed with death.

  

The wife further recollects how her husband used to come back home from the sea to be with her so eagerly and passionately. The simile 'chaste as a gull' suggests the husband's fidelity, strong love and affection towards the wife.

  

### **Stanza - 2**

  

Now that being dead, you were beyond detection,

and I need not be discreet, let us confess,

it was not love that married us no affection

but elders' persuasion, not even loneliness,

Recall how first you were so impatient and afraid

my eyes were open in the dark unlike in love,

trembling, lest in fear, you'll let me go a maid,

trembling on the other hand for my virginity.

  

- detection exposing something concealed earlier/finding out.
- discreet careful in one's speech about something confidential.
- confess disclose / reveal.
- persuasion encouragement / temptation /urging.
- Recall remember / recollect / bring a fact, event or situation to one's mind.
- impatient restless/ nervous/ anxious.
- trembling shaking or quivering as a result of anxiety or excitement.
- virginity-maidenhood/purity/virtue.

  

In the second stanza, the wife says that there is no need to conceal anything about the husband and herself as he is dead now. Nothing seems to be confidential for her at this moment. She decides to reveal the past events related to their marriage. It was a Sri Lankan traditional type of marriage as it was arranged by the parents. The parents would have persuaded them to marry without any pre acquaintance about each other before the marriage. It further suggests that their marriage was not based on love and affection like the modern marriages. As a matter of fact, they did not want to marry because they felt lonely without a partner. Thus the wife is very much authentic when disclosing the true nature of their marriage.

  

Next she goes on to reveal the novel experience they had at the first wedding night as a newly wedded couple. She is again authentic to describe the occasion very vividly and dramatically. It was an unfamiliar situation that made them embarrassed with anxiety and excitement. She recollects how the husband had mixed feelings like impatience and fear when facing the moment of consummation as a young man brought up in a rural fishing community. Even the wife had to face and pass the night being nervous and vigilant with open eyes more than tempted by love. In the last two lines, the wife brings out her mental confusion, uncertainties and fears at the first wedding night as it is more significant for a woman in a traditional marriage. It is the moment of a young maid attaining womanhood to assume the future role of a mother. Therefore, she had fear whether her husband would let her remain as a maid due to her excitement. On the other hand, It is the first consummation that she has to prove her purity and virtue of the character as a virgin not only to her husband but also to the parents and all the other relations.

  

Thus the poet depicts the true nature of arranged marriages that the husband and wife behave as two strangers until they develop their marital bond and mutual understanding with the passage of time.

  

### **Stanza - 3**

  

Three months the monsoon thrashed the sea, and you

remained at home; the sky cracked like a shell.

in thunder, the rain broke through, At last when pouring ceased and storm winds fell,

when gulls returned new- plumed and wild

when in our wind torn flamboyant

new buds broke, I was with child

  

- thrashed hit heavily.
- cracked - split / broke / made an explosive sound.
- shell - hard outer covering.
- pouring - heavy rain.
- ceased - stopped.
- new-plumed with new feathers.
- wild-looking natural.
- flamboyant a tree with red flowers.

  

In this stanza, the speaker shifts the topic continued in the second stanza at once so as to draw the attention of the reader to a natural phenomenon. It was the heavy monsoon rain thrashed the sea for three months. She remembers that her husband had to stop fishing during this stormy monsoon period and remain at home. It rained and thundered so heavily that the sky looked cracking like a shell. Then the speaker depicts the changes brought out by the natural cycle after ceasing the monsoon rain and stormy winds. Accordingly, seagulls returned with new feathers typical to their natural physical appearance. Even the flamboyant tree affected by the monsoon wind burst into bloom. At the end of the stanza, the speaker brings out the most surprising breaking news "I was with child."

  

### **Stanza - 4**

  

My face was wan while telling you and voices fell low,

and you seemed full of guilt and not to know

whether to repent or rejoice over the situation

You nodded at the ground and went to sea,

But soon I was to you more than God or temptation, and so were you to me

  

- wan - pale
- guilt wrong doing/feeling of worry that you have done something wrong.
- repent feel or express sincere regret
- rejoice feel or show great joy or delight
- nodded lower and raise one's head slightly
- temptation desire to do something

  

The first line of this stanza shows how the speaker felt very nervous and shy to convey the happy news of the conception to her husband. It is humorous that the husband also got embarrassed being guilty conscious whether he should feel happy or sad over this situation. He nodded at the ground and went to sea. His quiet response implies that he felt the added responsibility of the forthcoming fatherhood in addition to his present role as a husband. He would have thought of earning more to have a happy family life. They were hereafter not only bound to each other divinely and emotionally but also mutually and spiritually because of the child due to be born.

  

Thus, this stanza highlights how the marital bond between the two partners developed gradually and strengthened as a mutual and spiritual bond with the child of their own blood and flesh.

  

### **Stanza - 5**

  

Men come and go, some say that they understand,

Our children weep, the youngest thinks you're fast asleep:

theirs is fear and wonderment.

You had grown so familiar as my hand that I cannot with simple grief

assuage dismemberment.

  

- wonderment - astonishment/curiosity / surprise.
- grief-sorrow.
- assuage make less intense / lessen suffering.
- dismemberment-losing a part of the body.

  

This is the most moving stanza of the poem. It gives a very live and vivid picture of the funeral. We can visualize how the speaker is standing near the dead body of the beloved husband with her children. People attend the funeral to pay last respects. The children are weeping. It is really poignant because the youngest child thinks that his father is fast asleep. They have both fear and wonderment owing to the sudden loss of their loving father. They are not mature enough to understand the finality of death. By the time of the husband's death, both husband and wife had developed a strong mutual and spiritual bond between two of them. Therefore, she cannot merely mitigate her bereavement and dismemberment with simple grief.

  

### **Stanza - 6**

  

Outside the wind despoils of leaf

tree that it used to nurse;

once more the flamboyant is torn,

the sky cracks like a shell again,

so someone practical has gone

to make them bring the hearse

before the rain.

  

- despoils-steals/ violently removes.

  

Nature's cycle occurs as usual removing the leaves from the mother tree by strong wind. The flamboyant tree is torn again. Even the sky looks cracking like a shell due to thunder. It is natural that the wind violently removes the leaves from the tree in spite of its nursing and feeding them. In the same way the husband who fed them and nursed them has been taken away by the natural forces making her a widow. The last three lines suggest that the speaker has matured enough to face the stark reality that she has to continue life with children after the burial of the dead husband.

  

## **Themes of the poem**

  

The poem "The Fisherman Mourned by his Wife' is really an attempt of the Sri Lankan poet Patrick Fernando to provide an authentic portrayal of some aspects of Sri Lankan culture and dire poverty associated with rural background. For this purpose, he has selected rural fishing community which is more familiar to him as he was born and bred close to the south west coastal area of Sri Lanka.

  

Accordingly, the poet exposes the uncertain and unpredictable nature of fishing community. They have to struggle with vast sea for their livelihood. When they go to sea for fishing, their family members are not sure whether they come back home. So the men have to launch their boats and engage in fishing irrespective unimaginable dangers. On the other hand their wives and children have to wait till their return with restless and uncertain mind.

  

In the poem the speaker's husband had to embrace untimely death at young age leaving his wife and children. He did not have much experience about the harsh and bitter nature of fishing and death lurking in the sea. The speaker does not reveal us the cause for his death. But we can speculate that he would have been caught by a storm or met with an accident due to the breakdown of the boat On the other hand, most of the fishermen do not have a luxurious life style. In the poem, when the wife announced the husband about the news that she was with child, he kept silent and nodded at the ground and went to sea.

  

"whether to repent or rejoice over the situation

You nodded at the ground and went to sea"

  

His response can be interpreted in different ways. However, he would have thought of the challenge of running a family in their absolute poverty. Bringing up a child is not an easy task for a poor family because they have to live in a good healthy home environment. They find it difficult to afford for milk, medicine, sanitation and many other needs without sound economic security.

  

As we know only men go to sea for fishing. Women have to depend on the husbands for the financial needs. Therefore, an untimely death of husband is really an irreparable loss for them. That is why the woman in the poem says

  

"Outside the wind despoils of leaf

tree that it used to nurse;"

  

After the husband's death, she is alone with the children to bear up all the burdens as a widow. But she has to face the challenge with courage and strength because life should go on. Thus the poet brings out the true nature and dark reality of the fishermen's lives in a very convincing heart touching manner.

  

As far as the cultural aspects are concerned, the poet mainly draws our attention to the marriages that take place in rural fishing community. It is quite obvious that most of the marriages in Sri Lankan rural areas in the past were arranged marriages. As the speaker says the involvement and persuasion of the parents and elders could be seen in a marriage more than the personal wishes and affection of the partners. Thus, there is an apparent contrast between love marriages and arranged or proposed marriages. In a love marriage, affection towards each other is the guiding force to enter marriage. Consequently, the couple gets an ample opportunity to understand each other and develop a strong bond before their marriage. But especially the young girls in rural areas are not allowed by the parents to move freely with young men as the freedom enjoyed by the young boys and girls in city. On the other hand, the young men in fishing areas also do not get much more free time to get the attention and attraction of the young girls because they have to go to sea for fishing or help their fathers for various work related to fishing. Thus, they have rare opportunities to move with opposite sex because fishing is also done only by men. The speaker in the poem clearly says how they married without love and affection and pre- acquaintance like two strangers.

  

"it was not love that married us no affection

but elders' persuasion, not even loneliness,"

  

Not only love but also sex is an unfamiliar or prohibited matter before marriage in Sri Lankan rural areas unlike in European and city culture. Chastity and virginity are also highly admired and preserved in the rural communities giving prominent attention among the social ethics and norms. That is why even after marriage, the speaker and her husband hesitate and vacillate on the marriage bed for the first consummation.

  

"Recall how first you were so impatient and afraid

my eyes were open in the dark unlike in love,"

  

Wedding bed is not only for consummation but also for giving an opportunity to the bride to prove her virginity and purity of the character before her husband and other elders. Moreover, it is the turning point of a young girl's life to enter the womanhood from maidenhood. As a result, it is a blessing for a woman to attain sublime motherhood. The speaker in the poem seems to be well-aware of all these established cultural facts according to these lines.

  

"trembling, lest in fear, you'll let me go a maid, trembling on the other hand for my virginity"

  

Another aspect highlighted related to the rural marriages is that though a man and a woman marry as strangers, they gradually develop a strong mutual bond. Specially the birth of a child strengthens their marital bond making them parents with a sense of responsibility. This is evidently proved in the following lines in the poem.

  

"But soon I was to you more than God or temptation. and so were you to me"

  

This fact is further proved when the speaker says about her husband as follows

  

"chaste as a gull flying pointed home

in haste to be with me!"

  

In addition to the cultural and economic matters, the poet also conveys how people gain maturity through life experience. Life is not a bed of roses so that we cannot expect that everything happens in a perfect and cozy manner. Perhaps, we may have to face a lot of troubles and burdens beyond our control during our life time. Becoming a widow or a widower is the most unbearable challenge for any person during life time. In the poem, the speaker loses her husband untimely and unexpectedly and she who depended on husband is left alone with her children. Nevertheless, she does not wish to lament over the pathetic situation. She makes her mind to go ahead with strength and courage in spite of her emotional breakdown.

  

"that I cannot with simple grief assuage dismemberment."

  

She is also prepared to face the harsh reality of life through the experience she gained from her hard life from childhood to widowhood being a part and parcel of the fishing community.

  

## **Poetic Techniques**

  

- The Fisherman Mourned by his Wife is an elegy because it expresses the wife's sorrow for his dead husband.

  

- The poem is written in the form of a dramatic monologue and flashback. The wife seems to be standing before the husband's dead body with her children. While remaining there, she reminisces and recapitulates the events of the husband's fishing life, their marriage, first night of wedding, conceiving of the first child, announcing the news of the child to the husband etc. through flashback technique.

  

- Since all the above events are presented as different scenes with vivid and live actions and movements, there is a dramatic quality of the poem. So we can visualize all of them and form a tragic playlet in our mind. The poem also becomes cinematic because the narrator goes back to the past through flashback from the first stanza to fourth stanza and comes back to the present in the rest of the poem. Such changes of episodes related to past, present and future can be seen in cinema.

  

- As far as the form of the poem is concerned, there is no regularity of the number of the lines in each stanza and uniformity of the length of the lines. So the form of the poem is appropriate to reflect the confusion of the events depicted in the poem.

  

- The emotional breakdown of the poem is demonstrated through the rhyming scheme of the poem. The poem begins with a formal rhyming scheme in the first stanza despite its half rhymes. But it gradually becomes formless by the end of the poem.

  

- The poet employs an analogy to show the parallel between natural phenomenon and the events of human life. Because of the heavy monsoon rain, the husband had to stop fishing for three months and stay at home. When rain ceased, a lot of changes were visible in the environment such as the gulls with new feathers and the flamboyant tree with buds. At the same time, the woman was with child. Thus this analogy shows how nature brought fecundity to human life, birds and trees. It further coveys the universal truth that both living and non- living things of nature are inter-related and inter-dependent.

  

- In addition to this, analogy, the poet uses some other figures of speech throughout the poem.

  

## **Imagery**

  

- The husband's dead body kept in coffin and the speaker and the children are standing nearby
- People attend the funeral to pay last respects to the dead body
- The scenes of the first night of the wedding.
- Heavy monsoon rain
- Changes of nature like [Gulls returning with new plumes, flamboyant with new buds]
- Man going to the sea in and old-boat brown
- Wind despoils of leaf of the tree

  

## **Symbolism**

  

- Old-boat brown symbolizes dull and hard work of fishing/uncertainty of life going in an old boat

  

- Heavy rain with thundering at the beginning and end symbolizes phenomenon of nature as well as the renewal of the life cycle. It suggests that human life also should go on without stopping due to one person's death.

  

- Gulls with new plumes and flamboyant with new buds symbolize vitality and fertility,

  

## **Similes**

# **Animal Crackers By Richard De Zoysa [1958-1990 / Sri Lankan Poet]**

  

## **Note on the poet**

  

Richard De Zoysa was born on 18th March 1958 in Colombo, Sri Lanka and died on 18 February 1990. He was thirty two at the time of his death. He was of mixed ethnicity, his father Lucien de Zoysa a Sinhalese and his mother Dr. Manorani Saravanamuttu, a prominent medical surgeon from the minority Sri Lankan Tamil community. His mother's father, Manicasothy Saravanamuttu, was a prominent journalist and diplomat of Malaya.

  

He was educated at S. Thomas' College, Mount Lavinia. He was very talented in acting. He was adjudged Best Actor in the English medium at the national inter-school Shakespeare Drama Competition in 1972. He was a member of the Debating Team and Drama Society along with Chanaka Amaratunga.

  

Everybody had known Richard since he was an English TV news broadcaster, outstanding journalist with remarkable skills and of course of his charisma being an Actor in Cinema and the TV. In 1983, de Zoysa starred in Lester James Peries's film Yuganthaya with Gamini Fonseka. The role of socialist Malin Kabalana in the movie closely mirrored de Zoysa's own political ideology. He was the youngest to be assigned then as the bureau chief of the IPS (Inter Press Service) newly opened office in Lisbon, just one week before he was killed.

  

In the midnight of February 17, 1990, an armed group broke into their home, and abducted de Soyza and drove off without explanation while his Mother protested. On the following day 18th February de Soyza's lifeless body washed ashore and at Lunawa Beach Moratuwa, some 12 miles south of Colombo. Thus he was given an untimely tragic death by a death squad at that time.

  

Richard De Zoysa earned reputation as a Sri Lankan poet who penned in English. Some of his poems were written under pseudonym Angela de Silva. Apocalypse Soon, Animal Crackers, Rites of Passage, Good Friday- 1975, This Other Eden, The Poet, Gajagavannama, Birds, Beasts and relatives, Talking of Michael Angelo, Lepidoptera were some poems written by him. Some of these poems appear in the English Literary texts prescribed for the higher studies of English Literature.

  

Contemporary Politics, social injustice, racial conflicts are the main themes selected for his poems. He was very critical, satirical and radical in his views. Satire is the main technique employed in his poems. Also, the use of symbolism and animal imagery is another dominant feature in his poems.

  

## **Analysis of the lines**

  

### **Lines- I-4**

  

"Draw me a lion."

So I set my pen

to work. Produce a lazy, kindly beast...

Colour it yellow.

  

At the beginning of the poem, the little child, Dimitri whose name is mentioned below the poem, makes a request from the poet. "Draw me a lion'. It rather looks like a command. The relationship between Dimitri and the poet is not clear. Perhaps, the poet plays the role of a father or an elder relation at home. However, it is obvious that the speaker is an adult. The speaker responds to the order of the child immediately by setting the pen and drawing a lion.

  

He purposely drew the lion giving the traits of laziness and kindness so as to add some satirical flavor and symbolic layer. Then the child demands to colour it yellow. Thus, there is a very dramatic, poetic, aesthetic and live scene in this stanza.

  

### **Lines-5-11**

  

"Does it bite?"

"Sometimes,

but only when it's angry-

if you pull its tail

or say that it is just another cat...

But for the most part, indolent, biddable,

basking in the sun of ancient pride.

  

The poet blends both humour and satire in these lines. Dimitri asks the poet a very childish question whether the lion bites. It really adds humour and makes the scene more dramatic and live. The poet becomes more satirical by responding to the child's question in detail. According to his reply. the lion usually does not bite but, there are some occasions that it bites when getting angry due to pulling its tail or calling it another type of cat. In the last two lines, the poet makes an ironic remark that the lion is lazy, misleading and gullible. The adjective 'biddable' can be further defined as meek and submissive. The last line "basking in the sun of ancient pride" brings out more irony and satire through inference and symbolic implication. Accordingly, the lion symbolizes the Sinhalese who leisurely boast about their glorious past, but doing nothing worth at the present.

  

### **Lines-12-15**

  

(Outside, the sunlight seems a trifle dulled

and there's a distant roaring, like a pride

of lions, cross at being awakened

from long, deep sleep).

  

The poet depicts the weather condition outside the room as a little bit uninviting or unfavorable. At the same time, there is a roaring sound at distance like a group of lions being awakened from their long deep sleep. This animal image symbolically suggests that the Sinhalese race has awakened after falling into a deep slumber.

  

### **Lines-16-22**

  

Then

"Draw me a tiger."

Vision of a beast

compounded of Jim Corbett yarns

and Blake

stalks through my mind, blazing Nature's warning,

black bars on gold.

  

After making a comment on the outside situation within the brackets, the poet draws our attention to another request or a demand made by the child, 'Draw me a tiger'. The poet responds to the child's demand by visualizing such a beast found in Jim Corbett's stories and William Blake's poem "Tyger" to be drawn. Edward James Corbett was a British hunter, tracker and conservationist, author and naturalist, who hunted a large number of man-eating tigers and leopards in India. He wrote many interesting stories about the tigers he found and hunted.

  

The beast which has been reminisced by the poet from Jim Corbett's yarns and William Blake's Tyger, is now in his inward eye. The phrases 'blazing Nature's warning and black bars on gold' are associated with horror and terror of the beast in the speaker's visual image. Specially, the image of 'black bars on gold' connotes the L.T.T.E (The Tamil separatist militant group) which fought for an independent homeland (Tamil Eelam) in the North and the East.

  

### **Lines-23-29**

  

"DRAW!"

You turn and draw the gun

on me, as if to show

that three-years-old understands force majeure

and as you pull the silly plastic trigger

all hell breaks loose; quite suddenly the sky

is full of smoke and orange stripes of flame.

  

The word 'DRAW' is in block capital letters with an exclamation mark. It suggests that the poet gets a sudden shock due to the ferocious animal in his mind picture as well as the sudden action of the child drawing a plastic toy gun and aiming at him. He says that even a three year old child understands the superior power of a gun and the ability to make a person terrified.

  

At this moment, there is a sound outside as hell breaking loose and quite suddenly the sky is full of smoke and visible of rising up orange coloured flame.

  

### **Lines-30-31**

  

BUT HERE THERE ARE NO TIGERS

HERE THERE ARE ONLY LIONS.

  

The poet seems to convey an important message emphasizing it in capital letters. The adverb of place 'here' is referred to the south of Sri Lanka where there are no tigers. The tigers are referred to the rebels of the LTTE. The poet emphasizes the fact here there are only lions and the lions are referred to the Sinhalese or the Sinhala political leaders who wield power.

  

### **Lines-32-39**

  

And their jackals

run panting, rabid in the roaring's wake,

infecting all with madness as they pass

while My Lord

the Elephant sways in his shaded arbour,

wrinkles his ancient brows, and wonders-

if, did he venture out to quell this jungle-tide

of rising flame, he'd burn his tender feet.

  

These lines enhance the animal imagery introducing two more animals- the jackals and the elephant. The image of lions and jackals is a fine combination. The possessive pronoun "their" suggests a very close link between the lions and jackals. Who are these jackals? They are obviously the rowdies and hooligans who belong to the politically and racially backed up mobs specially during the black July, 1983. They gave a black mark to the ethnic peace and harmony in Sri Lanka by killing innocent Tamil civilians and looting their property wearing the fake mask of patriotism as a retaliatory attack for the killing of 13 Sri Lanka army soldiers in the north by a Tamil militant group.

  

The two lines:

  

**"run panting, rabid in the roaring's wake, infecting all with madness as they pass"** clearly suggest how the racist mobs played havoc turning the country into chaos.

  

Then 'My Lord' is referred to the head of the country, his Excellency the President at that time. The line the Elephant sways in his shaded arbour suggests how the president, governs the country residing at his shaded arbour which is the presidential residence. The phrase 'wrinkles his ancient brows' implies the president's old age. The president is wondering whether he should take any action to end this chaos caused by conflagration and communal violence of the racist mobs. He is hesitating and scared of taking the responsibility to stop the riots because it would burn his "tender feet". It means if he took an attempt to put down the violence, he would lose his political advantages.

  

### **Lines-40-42**

  

"Put down that gun. If you do, and you're good,

I'll draw a picture of an elephant.

A curious beast that you must understand..."

  

The poet coaxes the child to put down his gun. He promises him to draw a picture of an elephant. He asks the child to understand that it is a curious beast. The speaker throws his satire on the political party symbolized by the elephant because of its political stance during a very critical period in the history of Sri Lanka.

  

### **Line - 43**

  

DONT LOOK OUT THE WINDOW-

  

This line is written in capital letters to stress and order the child not to look out of the window. It is obvious that he does not want to spoil the child by looking at the ugly incidents happening outside.

  

### **Lines - 44-46**

  

"Just a party down the lane

a bonfire, and some fireworks, and they're burning

No, not a tiger just some silly cat."

  

The first two lines bring out a visual image of burning property of Tamil victims down the lane by a racist mob. They are not the property of the Tamil militants in the north but of the silly cats. It is very ironic and satirical that the racist mobs have taken innocent Tamil civilians [silly cats] as their victims, instead of the true culprits-tigers [Tamil militant groups like the LTTE] who ignited the sparks of racial fire in July, 1983, by killing army soldiers.

  

## **Themes**

  

Animal Crackers by Richard De Zoysa highlights the racial violence in July 1983, which darkened the lives of many Sri Lankans. As the communal disturbances took place in July 1983, it is called black July. The first seed of the disturbances was an incident occurred in the north on the 15th of October, in 1981. It was a clash between the Sri Lankan army and the militants in the north. There was another serious incident on Saturday 23rd July 1983 at 11-30 p.m. This violent incident created a dark atmosphere throughout the island and the violent activities continued from 24h to 29th of July.

  

The poem thereby, is based on this July racial violence. The poet seems to be analytical and satirical of the violence of 1983. He is highly sympathetic towards those who became victims of the violence.. It is true that there are number of socio-economic, political, historical, religious and linguistic factors behind them. The poet puts the blame on the governing party and the head of the government at that time for being inactive without exercising the power to suppress the escalating ethnic violence. Consequently, violence was responded with violence and hatred was responded with hatred but not with compassion. Rowdies, hooligans and politically and racially backed - up mobs in the south and the retaliatory terrorist groups in the north made the island a pool of blood and hell. The poet criticizes the politicians who backed the racial extremists to achieve their own political targets. Specially, they turned a blind eye towards the violence released against the Tamils in the south. Thus as the title of the poem "Animal Crackers" suggests the madness, bestiality and vulnerability displayed by people in black July, 1983.

  

## **Poetic Techniques**

  

### **Form/Structure -**

  

The poem is written in free verse form and it is appropriate to convey the chaotic situation in the poem. It is presented in the form of questioning and answering between a child and an adult. Most of the questions are asked by the child and the most of the answers, actions, thoughts and comments are related to the adult.

  

## **Title of the poem**

  

The title of the poem "Animal crackers' looks peculiar. According to British slang, the word The title of the poem of mad. Then the title "Animal crackers" implies the madness of animals. In the poem, the animals like lions, jackals, tigers and cats represent different ethnic groups or people in general. Therefore, the title can be defined as the madness of people or bestiality of people.

  

There is special remark like the sub- title of the poem **'For Dimitri, when he is old enough to understand.'** It is obvious that this poem was specially written for a child who was not then old enough to understand what this poem conveys. But the poet holds an optimistic view that Dimitri will understand the depth of the poem and the symbolic references in due course when she grows up. We wonder whether this Dimitri could be the child who appears in the poem and also the age three years is related to the child.

  

## **Imagery / Symbolism/Metaphor Irony / Satire**

  

The most dominant literary device in this poem is imagery. The poet uses animal imagery throughout the poem to describe the different ethnic groups, racist mobs, Tamil militant groups, rulers etc. The lion usually symbolizes bravery, courage, power and so on. Nevertheless, it is ironic and the lion in the poem displays the qualities of kindness, indolence and meekness. The lion is also the heraldic symbol of the Sinhalese. In that sense, the poet uses this animal image to create a sarcastic attitude towards the Sinhalese.

  

**"Produce a lazy kindly beast.."**

**"But for the most part indolent, biddable"**

  

The most powerful Tamil terrorist group in the north of Sri Lanka was the LTTE known as the tigers. The tiger as an animal, usually symbolizes ferocity, violence, evilness and so on. Therefore, the tiger in the poem is another animal image that is created to express the horror and terror of the LTTE. The Jackal usually symbolizes unscrupulous and immoral behavior. In the poem the jackals are the Sinhalese chauvinists, racist mobs and hooligans who were active in black July.

  

**"And their jackals**

**run panting, rabid in the roaring's wake,"**

  

Therefore, it is an appropriate symbol to create an animal image about them due to their killing of the Tamil civilians and burning their property. The elephant is the party symbol of the United National Party. The elephant in the poem thereby, depicts the political background and the role of the rulers during the communal disturbances in 1983 in Sri Lanka.

  

**'while My Lord**

**the Elephant sways in his shaded arbour,**

**wrinkles his ancient brows, and wonders'**

  

The silly cats symbolize the innocent ordinary Tamil people who became victims of the racial violence.

  

**No, not a tiger-just some silly cat."**

  

Further, the image of 'a bonfire, and some fire works....' seems to suggest the vulnerability of the innocent Tamil civilians who are regarded as 'silly cats". It also suggests how the rioting mobs symbolically celebrate the annihilation of the Tamils by erecting a bonfire.

  

The poet uses colour image at the beginning of the poem. When the poet was drawing the lion, the child asked him to colour it yellow. **"Colour it yellow"** This color image is associated with the qualities of inactiveness, sickness and meekness. The poet wants to convey that even the Sinhalese

  

The word 'draw' is repeated throughout the poem and it is a metaphor for comparing the innocent action of drawing a picture to the action of drawing a gun for a destructive and violent purpose.

  

## **Use of satire**

  

Satire is another technique used in the poem. The lion is described as a lazy kindly beast. Further, the two adjectives indolent and biddable are used to give the characteristics of the lion. Thus, symbolically, the poet is satirical about the Sinhalese who have lost the sublime qualities like heroism, bravery and glory. Even the role of the head of the country is satirized since he behaves like an irresponsible and opportunistic person at such a critical situation.

  

## **Use of Language**

  

The use of conversational language is very effective to boost the dramatic and live quality of the poem. The child makes his requests in the form of demands and commands and it is the nature of the small children before adults.

  

'Draw me a lion'

  

'Colour it yellow'

  

'Draw me a tiger'

  

Then the child's handling of the toy gun marks the end of the drawing lesson and beginning of the depiction of violent atmosphere. The poet looks serious and starts giving commands to the child **'Put down that gun' / 'DON'T LOOK OUT OF THE WINDOW**

  

In additions to the language functions and expressions like requests and commands, there are also comments, questions, insinuations, inferences and so on.

  

The use of capital letters helps the poet to highlight the comments, commands and emphasize his observations.

  

The use of spoken words contributes to the dramatic quality of the poem. We can easily visualize a child and an adult and their interaction here. The poet's elaborate comments in response to the child's simple commands are like brush strokes (although he uses a pen) used to define the animal's features. In other words, he creates verbal pictures through his comments.
